## Big ideas

- **EVO · Evolution.** Life changes over generations; shared ancestry explains the unity of life, and selection, drift and gene flow acting on variation explain its diversity.
- **ENE · Energetics.** Living things capture, store and spend energy and exchange matter with their surroundings to grow, reproduce and hold a steady internal state.
- **IST · Information storage and transmission.** Heritable information in DNA (and RNA) is copied, passed on, expressed and regulated; cells and organisms also send and receive signals that change what they do.
- **SYI · Systems interactions.** Parts interact at every level — molecules, cells, organisms, populations, ecosystems — and the behaviour of the whole emerges from, and constrains, those interactions.

## Practices

- **P1 · Concept explanation.** Explain a biological concept, process or model in words, both in general and applied to a concrete situation.
- **P2 · Visual representations.** Read, explain and use diagrams, models, pedigrees, trees and other pictures of biological systems, and say what they leave out.
- **P3 · Questions and methods.** Pose a testable question, pick variables and controls, and judge whether an experimental design can answer it.
- **P4 · Representing and describing data.** Build and read graphs and tables, and describe the trends, patterns and relationships they show.
- **P5 · Statistical tests and data analysis.** Do the calculation — ratios, rates, probabilities, frequencies, chi-square, standard error — and decide what the number supports.
- **P6 · Argumentation.** Make a claim, back it with evidence and reasoning, and predict the effect of a change to a biological system.

## Unit 1 · Chemistry of Life

Big ideas: SYI, ENE, IST

### 1.1 Structure of water and hydrogen bonding



- ○ **BI-1.1.A** (SYI-1.A) Explain how water's polarity lets it hydrogen-bond to other water molecules and to polar or charged solutes.
  - EK: O pulls electrons from H, leaving δ− on O and δ+ on each H; each water can H-bond to up to four neighbours.
  - P1
- ○ **BI-1.1.B** (SYI-1.A) Connect cohesion, adhesion, high specific heat, ice floating and solvent power to hydrogen bonding and to a biological consequence.
  - EK: Cohesion + adhesion drive transpiration pull; high specific heat buffers temperature; open-lattice ice insulates water below.
  - Needs: BI-1.1.A
  - P1 · P6

### 1.2 Elements of life



- ○ **BI-1.2.A** (ENE-1.A) Identify which elements each macromolecule class contains and where C, N, P and S enter biological molecules.

  - P1
- ○ **BI-1.2.B** (ENE-1.A) Predict which macromolecule class picks up a ³⁵S, ³²P or ¹⁵N label.
  - EK: ³⁵S marks protein only; ³²P marks nucleic acid (and phospholipid); ¹⁵N marks protein and nucleic acid.

### 1.3 Introduction to biological macromolecules

- ○ **BI-1.5.A** (SYI-1.C) Describe the four levels of protein structure and the interactions that hold each.

- ○ **BI-3.3.C** (ENE-1.G) Read saturation and inhibition from rate-versus-substrate-concentration curves.

### 9.5 Immune and lymphatic systems



- ○ **BI-9.5.A** (DAT Bio · systems · immunological/lymphatic) Distinguish innate from adaptive immunity.

- ○ **BI-9.5.B** (DAT Bio · systems · immunological/lymphatic) Explain humoral and cell-mediated responses, MHC presentation and immunological memory.

### 9.6 Digestive and urinary systems

- ○ **BI-10.1.A** (DAT Bio · genetics · developmental mechanisms) Order fertilisation, cleavage, blastulation, gastrulation and neurulation and describe each.

- ○ **BI-10.3.A** (DAT Bio · diversity · viruses) Describe virus structure and compare lytic, lysogenic and retroviral life cycles.

### 10.5 Eukaryotic diversity



- ○ **BI-10.5.A** (DAT Bio · diversity · fungi/protista) Distinguish fungi, protists, plants and animals by wall, nutrition and life cycle.
