## Big ideas

- **SYS · Choose the system first.** Every analysis starts by drawing a boundary. What is inside fixes which forces are internal, which energies are stored, and which quantities are conserved; a system can be treated as one point at its center of mass when its insides don't matter.
- **INT · Interactions are forces, and forces come from fields.** Objects act on each other in pairs (contact, gravitational, electric, magnetic). The net external force sets how a system's motion changes; at a distance the interaction is carried by a field that exists whether or not a test object is there.
- **CONS · Conserved quantities shortcut the details.** Energy, momentum, angular momentum and charge are neither created nor destroyed; they move between objects and forms. When the system is chosen so nothing crosses its boundary, before = after, no matter how messy the middle.
- **CHG · Change has a rate and an accumulation.** Velocity, acceleration, current and power are rates; displacement, work, impulse and charge are accumulations. Slopes and areas on graphs, and derivatives and integrals in the calculus layer, move between them.
- **WAVE · Waves carry energy and superpose.** Oscillations that propagate move energy without moving matter; overlapping waves add, which produces interference, standing waves, diffraction and beats.
- **QUANT · At small scales energy comes in packets.** Light arrives as photons of energy hf, matter has a wavelength h/p, atoms have discrete energy levels, and nuclei turn mass into energy.

## Practices

- **1A · Create diagrams.** Draw a diagram, table or schematic (motion diagram, free-body diagram, energy bar chart, ray diagram, circuit) that represents the situation.
- **1B · Graph data.** Plot a quantitative graph with scales and units, linearise where useful, and read slopes and intercepts.
- **1C · Sketch qualitative graphs.** Sketch the shape of a graph that a model predicts (x–t, v–t, U–x, I–t) without numbers.
- **2A · Derive an expression.** Combine laws and definitions symbolically to get the quantity asked for in terms of the givens.
- **2B · Calculate with units.** Compute or estimate a number with correct units and significant figures.
- **2C · Compare.** Compare a quantity across situations, times or locations and say which is larger and why.
- **2D · Predict from functional dependence.** Say how a quantity changes by what factor when another is changed (doubles, quarters, unchanged).
- **3A · Design an experiment.** Propose what to measure, with what, and how to reduce uncertainty to answer a question.
- **3B · Apply the right model.** Pick the law, definition or model that fits the situation and its validity limits, and use it to make a claim.
- **3C · Justify with evidence.** Support or refute a claim with physical principles, data or a representation.

## Unit 1 · Kinematics

Big ideas: CHG, SYS

### 1.1 Scalars and vectors in one dimension



- ○ **PH-1.1.A** (P1 1.1.A) Classify a quantity as a scalar or a vector and state a vector's magnitude and direction.
  - EK: A vector has magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, force); a scalar has magnitude only (distance, speed, mass, energy).
  - 3B
- ○ **PH-1.1.B** (P1 1.1.B) Add collinear vectors using a sign convention for direction.
  - EK: In 1-D, pick a positive direction; opposite vectors carry opposite signs and add algebraically.
  - Needs: PH-1.1.A
  - 2B

### 1.2 Displacement, velocity and acceleration



- ○ **PH-1.2.A** (P1 1.2.A) Calculate displacement and distinguish it from distance travelled.
  - EK: Δx = x_f − x_i depends only on start and end points; distance is the total path length.
  - Needs: PH-1.1.B
  - 2B
- ○ **PH-1.2.B** (P1 1.2.B) Calculate average velocity and average acceleration over a time interval.
  - EK: v_avg = Δx/Δt; a_avg = Δv/Δt; acceleration opposite to velocity means slowing down.
  - Needs: PH-1.2.A
  - 2B

### 1.3 Representing motion



- ○ **PH-1.3.A** (P1 1.3.A) Read instantaneous velocity and acceleration from slopes, and displacement and Δv from areas, on motion graphs.
  - EK: Slope of x–t is v; slope of v–t is a; area under v–t is Δx; area under a–t is Δv.
  - Needs: PH-1.2.B
  - 1C · 3B
- ○ **PH-1.3.B** (P1 1.3.A) Solve a constant-acceleration problem with the kinematic equations.
  - EK: v = v₀ + at; x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²; v² = v₀² + 2aΔx; pick the one missing the unknown you don't need.
  - Needs: PH-1.3.A
  - 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-1.3.C** (P1 1.3.A) Analyze vertical free fall as motion with constant acceleration g downward.
  - EK: Near Earth's surface a = −9.8 m/s² (up positive) throughout the flight, including at the top where v = 0.
  - Needs: PH-1.3.B
  - 2B · 3B
- ○ **PH-1.3.D** (P1 1.3.A) Translate between a narrative, a motion diagram and consistent x–t, v–t and a–t sketches.
  - EK: The three graphs must agree: where v–t crosses zero, x–t has a turning point; constant a gives a straight v–t and a parabolic x–t.
  - Needs: PH-1.3.A
  - 1A · 1C

### 1.4 Reference frames and relative motion



- ○ **PH-1.4.A** (P1 1.4.A, P1 1.4.B) Calculate an object's velocity as measured by observers in different inertial frames moving along one line.
  - EK: v_A rel C = v_A rel B + v_B rel C; all inertial observers measure the same acceleration.
  - Needs: PH-1.2.B
  - 2B · 3B

### 1.5 Vectors and motion in two dimensions



- ○ **PH-1.5.A** (P1 1.5.A) Resolve a vector into perpendicular components and rebuild a vector from its components.
  - EK: A_x = A cos θ, A_y = A sin θ (θ from +x); |A| = √(A_x² + A_y²), θ = tan⁻¹(A_y/A_x) with quadrant check.
  - Needs: PH-1.1.A
  - 2B
- ○ **PH-1.5.B** (P1 1.5.B) Analyze a horizontally launched projectile by treating horizontal and vertical motion independently.
  - EK: Ignoring air: a_x = 0, a_y = −g; the two directions share only the time.
  - Needs: PH-1.5.A, PH-1.3.C
  - 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-1.5.C** (P1 1.5.B) Calculate time of flight, maximum height and range of a projectile launched at an angle.
  - EK: v_y = 0 at the top; on level ground R = v₀² sin 2θ / g, maximal at 45°.
  - Needs: PH-1.5.B
  - 2A · 2B · 2D
- ○ **PH-1.5.D** (P1 1.5.B, P1 1.4.B) Add relative velocities in two dimensions (boat crossing a river, plane in a crosswind).
  - EK: Relative velocities add as vectors; add components, then find magnitude and heading.
  - Needs: PH-1.4.A, PH-1.5.A

## Unit 2 · Force and translational dynamics

Big ideas: INT, SYS

### 2.1 Systems and center of mass



- ○ **PH-2.1.A** (P1 2.1.A) Choose a system and sort the forces on it into internal and external.
  - EK: Internal forces cancel in pairs; only external forces change the motion of the system's center of mass.
  - 1A · 3B
- ○ **PH-2.1.B** (P1 2.1.B) Calculate the center of mass of a system of point masses.
  - EK: x_cm = Σm_i x_i / Σm_i (each axis separately); it lies closer to the heavier parts.
  - Needs: PH-2.1.A
  - 2B

### 2.2 Forces and free-body diagrams



- ○ **PH-2.2.A** (P1 2.2.A) Identify each force on an object as an interaction with a named other object, contact or at a distance.
  - EK: Every force has an agent; no agent, no force ("force of motion" is not a force).
  - 3B
- ○ **PH-2.2.B** (P1 2.2.B) Draw a free-body diagram with every force on the object, from its point of action, in the correct direction.
  - EK: Normal force is perpendicular to the surface, tension along the string away from the object, gravity toward Earth's center.
  - Needs: PH-2.2.A
  - 1A

### 2.3 Newton's third law



- ○ **PH-2.3.A** (P1 2.3.A) Identify third-law force pairs and distinguish them from balanced forces on one object.
  - EK: A third-law pair is equal and opposite, acts on two different objects and is the same type of force.

  - 3B · 3C

### 2.4 Newton's first law



- ○ **PH-2.4.A** (P1 2.4.A) Find an unknown force (tension, normal, applied) on an object in translational equilibrium.
  - EK: Constant velocity (including rest) ⇔ ΣF = 0; write ΣF_x = 0 and ΣF_y = 0 separately.
  - Needs: PH-2.2.B, PH-1.5.A
  - 1A · 2A · 2B

### 2.5 Newton's second law



- ○ **PH-2.5.A** (P1 2.5.A) Apply ΣF = ma to one object to find its acceleration or an unknown force.
  - EK: a = ΣF/m points along the net force; the kinematics then follows from Unit 1.
  - Needs: PH-2.2.B, PH-1.3.B
  - 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-2.5.B** (P1 2.5.A) Find the acceleration and string tension of connected objects (Atwood machine, blocks joined by a string).
  - EK: Treat the whole as one system for a, then isolate one object for the tension; an ideal string has one tension and one |a|.
  - Needs: PH-2.5.A, PH-2.3.A, PH-2.1.A
  - 1A · 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-2.5.C** (P1 2.5.A) Analyze an object on an incline by resolving gravity along and perpendicular to the surface.
  - EK: Along the incline mg sin θ, into it mg cos θ; on a frictionless incline a = g sin θ.
  - Needs: PH-2.5.A, PH-1.5.A
  - 1A · 2A

### 2.6 Gravitational force



- ○ **PH-2.6.A** (P1 2.6.A) Calculate the gravitational force between two masses with Newton's law of gravitation.
  - EK: F = Gm₁m₂/r², r center to center; doubling r quarters F.
  - 2B · 2D
- ○ **PH-2.6.B** (P1 2.6.A) Calculate the gravitational field strength g at a planet's surface or at altitude.
  - EK: g = GM/r²; weight mg is the gravitational force, and g is also the free-fall acceleration.
  - Needs: PH-2.6.A
  - 2A · 2B

### 2.7 Kinetic and static friction



- ○ **PH-2.7.A** (P1 2.7) Calculate kinetic friction on a sliding object and the acceleration it produces.
  - EK: f_k = μ_k N, opposite to the relative sliding velocity; N is found from the perpendicular force balance, not assumed to be mg.
  - Needs: PH-2.5.A
  - 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-2.7.B** (P1 2.7) Decide whether static friction can hold an object and find the friction force actually acting.
  - EK: f_s ≤ μ_s N: static friction is whatever the balance needs, up to the maximum; slipping starts when the need exceeds μ_s N.
  - Needs: PH-2.4.A, PH-2.7.A
  - 2C · 3B

### 2.8 Spring forces



- ○ **PH-2.8.A** (P1 2.8) Apply Hooke's law to find a spring force, stretch or spring constant, including k from a force–extension graph.
  - EK: F_s = −kΔx, restoring toward equilibrium; k is the slope of F vs Δx.
  - Needs: PH-2.4.A
  - 1B · 2B

### 2.9 Circular motion



- ○ **PH-2.9.A** (P1 2.9) Calculate centripetal acceleration and identify which real force supplies the net inward force.
  - EK: a_c = v²/r toward the center; centripetal is a role (tension, friction, gravity, normal), never an extra force on the FBD.
  - Needs: PH-2.5.A, PH-1.5.A
  - 1A · 2B
- ○ **PH-2.9.B** (P1 2.9) Apply ΣF_r = mv²/r to a car on a flat curve, a vertical loop or a conical pendulum.
  - EK: Flat curve: μ_s mg ≥ mv²/r; top of a loop: N + mg = mv²/r, minimum speed √(gr).
  - Needs: PH-2.9.A, PH-2.7.B
  - 1A · 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-2.9.C** (P1 2.9, P1 6.6) Derive the speed and period of a circular orbit from gravity as the centripetal force.
  - EK: GMm/r² = mv²/r ⇒ v = √(GM/r), T² = 4π²r³/(GM); orbital speed is independent of the satellite's mass.
  - Needs: PH-2.9.A, PH-2.6.B
  - 2A · 2D

## Unit 3 · Work, energy and power

Big ideas: CONS, SYS

### 3.1 Translational kinetic energy



- ○ **PH-3.1.A** (P1 3.1) Calculate translational kinetic energy and predict how it scales with speed and mass.
  - EK: K = ½mv² is a scalar, never negative; doubling v quadruples K.
  - Needs: PH-1.2.B
  - 2B · 2D

### 3.2 Work



- ○ **PH-3.2.A** (P1 3.2) Calculate the work done by a constant force over a straight displacement.
  - EK: W = Fd cos θ; a force perpendicular to the motion (normal, centripetal) does no work; friction on a sliding object does negative work.
  - Needs: PH-1.5.A, PH-2.2.B
  - 2B
- ○ **PH-3.2.B** (P1 3.2) Find work as the area under a force–position graph, including the work to stretch a spring.
  - EK: W = area under F–x; for a spring from 0 to x, W = ½kx².
  - Needs: PH-3.2.A, PH-2.8.A
  - 1B · 2B
- ○ **PH-3.2.C** (P1 3.2) Apply the work–energy theorem to find a speed or a force.
  - EK: W_net = ΔK; the net work is the sum of the work done by each force.
  - Needs: PH-3.2.A, PH-3.1.A
  - 2A · 2B

### 3.3 Potential energy



- ○ **PH-3.3.A** (P1 3.3) Calculate gravitational potential energy near Earth and elastic potential energy of a spring for a system.
  - EK: U_g = mgh (reference level is a choice; only ΔU matters); U_s = ½kx²; potential energy belongs to a system of interacting objects.
  - Needs: PH-3.2.B
  - 2B
- ○ **PH-3.3.B** (P1 3.3) Calculate the gravitational potential energy of two widely separated masses.
  - EK: U_G = −Gm₁m₂/r with U = 0 at infinite separation; it rises toward zero as r grows.
  - Needs: PH-3.3.A, PH-2.6.A
  - 2B · 2D

### 3.4 Conservation of energy



- ○ **PH-3.4.A** (P1 3.4) Apply conservation of mechanical energy to find a speed, height or spring compression.
  - EK: With only conservative forces doing work inside the system, K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f.
  - Needs: PH-3.3.A, PH-3.2.C
  - 1A · 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-3.4.B** (P1 3.4) Account for friction and external work in an energy balance.
  - EK: ΔK + ΔU + ΔE_th = W_ext; sliding friction converts f_k d into thermal energy.
  - Needs: PH-3.4.A, PH-2.7.A
  - 1A · 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-3.4.C** (P1 3.4, P1 6.6) Calculate the escape speed from a planet's surface.
  - EK: ½mv_esc² − GMm/R = 0 ⇒ v_esc = √(2GM/R), √2 times the circular orbital speed at the same r.
  - Needs: PH-3.4.A, PH-3.3.B
  - 2A · 2B

### 3.5 Power



- ○ **PH-3.5.A** (P1 3.5) Calculate average power and the instantaneous power delivered by a force.
  - EK: P_avg = W/Δt = ΔE/Δt; P = Fv cos θ; 1 W = 1 J/s.
  - Needs: PH-3.2.A
  - 2B

### 4.4 Elastic and inelastic collisions



- ○ **PH-4.4.A** (P1 4.4) Classify a collision as elastic or inelastic by comparing kinetic energy before and after.

- ○ **PH-4.4.B** (P1 4.4) Find the final velocity and the kinetic energy lost in a perfectly inelastic collision.

- ○ **PH-4.4.C** (P1 4.4) Find both final velocities in a one-dimensional elastic collision.

## Unit 5 · Torque and rotational dynamics

Big ideas: INT, CHG

### 5.1 Rotational kinematics



- ○ **PH-5.1.A** (P1 5.1) Solve constant-angular-acceleration problems with the rotational kinematic equations.
  - EK: ω = ω₀ + αt; θ = ω₀t + ½αt²; ω² = ω₀² + 2αΔθ — the linear equations with θ, ω, α.

### 5.2 Connecting linear and rotational motion



- ○ **PH-5.2.A** (P1 5.2) Convert between angular and linear quantities for a point on a rotating object.
  - EK: s = rθ, v = rω, a_t = rα (θ in radians); every point shares ω, speed grows with r.
  - Needs: PH-5.1.A
  - 2B · 2C

### 5.3 Torque



- ○ **PH-5.3.A** (P1 5.3) Calculate the torque of a force about an axis and its sense of rotation.
  - EK: τ = rF sin θ = r⊥F (lever arm); counterclockwise positive by convention.

  - 1A · 2B

### 5.4 Rotational inertia



- ○ **PH-5.4.A** (P1 5.4) Calculate the rotational inertia of point masses and combine given inertias of rigid shapes.
  - EK: I = Σm_i r_i²; inertias about the same axis add; mass far from the axis costs the most.
  - Needs: PH-2.1.B
  - 2B · 2C
- ○ **PH-5.4.B** (P1 5.4) Shift a rotational inertia to a parallel axis.
  - EK: I = I_cm + Md²; I is smallest about an axis through the center of mass.
  - Needs: PH-5.4.A
  - 2B

### 5.5 Rotational equilibrium



- ○ **PH-5.5.A** (P1 5.5) Find unknown forces on a beam, ladder or sign in static equilibrium.

  - Needs: PH-5.3.A, PH-2.4.A

### 5.6 Newton's second law in rotational form



- ○ **PH-5.6.A** (P1 5.6) Apply Στ = Iα to find the angular acceleration of a rigid object.
  - EK: α = Στ/I about a fixed axis or the center of mass.
  - Needs: PH-5.3.A, PH-5.4.A, PH-2.5.A
  - 2A · 2B
- ○ **PH-5.6.B** (P1 5.6) Solve a massive-pulley problem by coupling the translational and rotational equations.
  - EK: Each hanging mass: ΣF = ma; pulley: (T₁ − T₂)R = Iα; no-slip: a = Rα. Tensions differ when the pulley has mass.
  - Needs: PH-5.6.A, PH-5.2.A, PH-2.5.B
  - 1A · 2A
