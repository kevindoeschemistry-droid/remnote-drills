## Big ideas

- **DRAG · Speed is propulsion against resistance.** Water drag grows with v² and the power to beat it with v³, so a small cut in drag buys more speed than a large gain in fitness. Every technique choice is judged by what it does to net force.
- **PUSH · You move forward by pushing water back.** Propulsion is Newton's third law — hands, forearms, feet and the body wave accelerate water backward. The evidence says it is mostly drag-type force on a well-angled hand and forearm, not Bernoulli lift from sculling.
- **RHYTHM · Velocity = stroke rate × distance per stroke.** Every swim is a trade between how often you cycle and how far each cycle carries you; fatigue, pacing and technique all show up as moves in that SR × DPS plane, and timing between arms, legs and breath decides where you can sit in it.
- **RULES · The rules define the strokes.** A stroke is whatever SW 4–10 allow. Legality bounds technique (two-hand touches, 15 m, one pull-down), and knowing exactly where the line sits lets you use everything up to it.
- **ENERGY · Energy systems set what pace lasts how long.** Phosphagen, glycolytic and aerobic supply overlap with different rates and capacities; each race distance has its own mix, and training adapts the system it stresses.
- **LOOP · Measure, plan, adjust.** Splits, stroke counts, CSS tests and video turn feel into numbers; a race plan, a season plan and a set are hypotheses that the numbers confirm or overturn.

## Practices

- **P1 · Observe and describe.** Say exactly what the body is doing — phase, timing, angle, count — from video, a split sheet or feel, before judging it.
- **P2 · Explain the mechanism.** Connect a technique or training choice to the physics (drag, impulse, momentum) or physiology (energy system, adaptation) that makes it work.
- **P3 · Apply the rule.** Make the official's call on a described action, name the SW (or USA/USMS) rule, and know where the Masters rules differ.
- **P4 · Compute.** Velocity, pace, splits, SR, DPS, CSS, intervals and set totals, with units and course (SCY / SCM / LCM) kept straight.
- **P5 · Diagnose and correct.** From a symptom, find the root fault, choose the drill or cue that fixes it, and say what evidence would show it worked.
- **P6 · Plan and decide.** Build a race plan, a set, a week or a season to a goal, and predict what changes when one parameter changes.

## Unit 1 · Hydrodynamics and propulsion

Big ideas: DRAG, PUSH

### 1.1 Types of drag and how they scale

Book: naemi-2010 §drag components; toussaint-1994 §drag factor

- ○ **SW-1.1.A** Classify a described resistance (rough suit, wide kick, bow wave at the surface) as skin-friction, form (pressure) or wave drag.
  - EK: Skin friction depends on surface; form drag on frontal area, shape and alignment; wave drag on speed and depth below the surface.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-1.1.B** Compute how drag and the power to overcome it change when velocity changes by a given factor.
  - EK: D = ½ρC_dAv²; P = Dv ∝ v³ — 5% faster costs ≈ 16% more power.
  - P4
- ○ **SW-1.1.C** Explain why gliding and kicking underwater can be faster than swimming at the surface.
  - EK: Wave drag is large at the surface and falls off steeply with depth (most of it gone by roughly 0.5 m).
  - P2

### 1.2 Streamline and glide

Book: naemi-2010 §glide efficiency, §added mass

- ○ **SW-1.2.A** Describe a tight streamline and explain each element's effect on drag (stacked hands, arms squeezing ears, head neutral, pointed toes).
  - EK: Each element reduces frontal area or flow separation; a raised head adds form drag.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-1.2.B** Explain why a glide decelerates and when to start kicking off the wall.
  - EK: Glide velocity decays as drag acts on body plus added mass; start kicking when glide speed drops toward kicking speed, not after.
  - P2 · P6

### 1.3 How propulsion is made

Book: sanders-lift §Counsilman 1971, §Cappaert & Rushall 1994; toussaint-1994 §propelling efficiency

- ○ **SW-1.3.A** Explain propulsion as momentum given to water (Newton III) and say why a high-elbow catch helps.
  - EK: Thrust = rate of backward momentum given to water; a vertical forearm adds paddle area facing backward early in the pull.
  - P2
- ○ **SW-1.3.B** Contrast Counsilman's lift (Bernoulli sculling) model with the drag-dominant view and state what the evidence supports.
  - EK: 3-D studies of elite swimmers (Cappaert, Rushall, Sanders) found drag > lift through most of the pull in all strokes but breaststroke; hand paths are straighter than once taught, curvature mostly from body roll.
  - P2
- ○ **SW-1.3.C** Explain propelling efficiency and why it can matter more than aerobic power.
  - EK: Part of the work goes into moving water, not the body; Toussaint & Hollander modelled efficiency gains as worth more than a 10% rise in aerobic or anaerobic power.
  - P2
- ○ **SW-1.3.D** Explain how the dolphin kick produces thrust from a whole-body wave.
  - EK: A wave travelling head-to-toe with growing amplitude whips the feet; thrust comes mostly on the downkick, with the upkick also propulsive when the feet press water backward.
  - P2

## Unit 2 · Freestyle and backstroke technique principles

Big ideas: PUSH, DRAG, RHYTHM

### 2.1 Freestyle

Book: asca §L2 Stroke School; world-aquatics-rules §SW 5

- ○ **SW-2.1.A** Describe the phases of the freestyle arm stroke (entry, catch, pull, push, recovery) and the purpose of each.
  - EK: Propulsive force is concentrated from catch to push; entry and recovery set up the next pull and the body line.
  - P1
- ○ **SW-2.1.B** Distinguish catch-up, front-quadrant and opposition timing and match each to distance.
  - EK: More overlap ⇒ longer DPS, lower SR (distance); less overlap ⇒ continuous propulsion, higher SR (sprint).
  - P1 · P6
- ○ **SW-2.1.C** Diagnose a freestyle fault from a description (crossover entry, dropped elbow, scissor kick on breath, lifted head) and choose the correction.
  - EK: Each fault either adds drag or wastes impulse; fix the root cause, usually upstream in head or entry.
  - P5

### 2.2 Backstroke

Book: asca §L2 Stroke School; world-aquatics-rules §SW 6

- ○ **SW-2.2.A** Describe the backstroke catch (little-finger entry, bent-arm pull) and why rotation lets the arm catch deep enough.
  - EK: Rotation of ~45° each side keeps the pull under the body instead of sweeping wide at the surface.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-2.2.B** Diagnose a backstroke fault (sitting hips, overreaching past the head, flat body) and choose the correction.
  - EK: Head still and level, hips at the surface; entry in line with the shoulder.
  - P5

## Unit 3 · Butterfly in depth

Big ideas: PUSH, RHYTHM, DRAG, RULES

### 3.1 Body wave and the axis at the hips

Book: usms-fly §body line; asca §L2 Stroke School

- ○ **SW-3.1.A** Describe the butterfly body wave and say where the axis sits and what moves forward vs up and down.
  - EK: The wave pivots near the hips; hips stay at or near the surface; the chest presses to lift the hips; the head leads the wave.
  - P1
- ○ **SW-3.1.B** Explain why over-undulation slows the stroke.
  - EK: Large vertical excursion adds form and wave drag, deepens the catch and lowers tempo; energy goes up and down instead of forward.
  - P2

### 3.2 Arms, kick and their timing

Book: usms-fly §kick timing, §hand entry; world-aquatics-rules §SW 8.2, §SW 8.3

- ○ **SW-3.2.A** State when each of the two kicks per cycle happens and what each does.
  - EK: Kick 1 at hand entry lifts the hips and balances; kick 2 as the hands push and exit is the more propulsive and lifts the shoulders for recovery.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-3.2.B** Describe the fly pull path from entry to exit and where the high-elbow catch is set.
  - EK: Hands enter in front of the shoulders, set a high-elbow catch just outside them, pull under the body and push past the hips.
  - P1
- ○ **SW-3.2.C** Diagnose kick-kick-pull timing from a description and choose the correction.
  - EK: Both kicks before the catch force an arched back and a downward push; cue one kick, then let the small second kick come as the hands exit — 'kick the hands in, kick the hands out'.
  - P5

### 3.3 Breathing and recovery

Book: usms-fly §breathing

- ○ **SW-3.3.A** Describe when the breath starts and ends relative to the pull and recovery.
  - EK: Head lift begins with the pull, the inhale comes as the mouth clears at the push, and the face returns before the hands enter; chin stays near the waterline.
  - P1
- ○ **SW-3.3.B** Diagnose late breathing or a high head lift from a description and choose the correction.
  - EK: A breath that starts at the end of the pull locks neck and shoulders and drops the hips; time the head with the catch and breathe forward, low.
  - P5
- ○ **SW-3.3.C** Choose a breathing pattern (every stroke, 1-up-1-down, 2-down) for a race distance and justify it from drag and oxygen cost.
  - EK: Each breath adds drag and disrupts the wave; fewer breaths for 50s, regular breathing to keep the 200 aerobic.
  - P6

### 3.4 Fly faults and fatigue

Book: usms-fly §common faults

- ○ **SW-3.4.A** Diagnose a fly fault from a description (crashing entry, dropped elbow, bent-knee kick, arms dragging through recovery) and choose the correction.
  - EK: Crashing entry ⇒ place hands and let chest submerge; dropped elbow ⇒ pitch palms and forearms back with fingertips down; bent knees ⇒ think hips at the surface, not 'kick harder'.
  - P5
- ○ **SW-3.4.B** Predict how fly technique degrades over the last 50 of a 200 and which cue protects speed best.
  - EK: Recovery drops, hips sink, kick 2 disappears, SR falls; holding hip height and kick 2 protects DPS.
  - Needs: SW-7.2.B
  - P2 · P6

### 3.5 Butterfly rules

Book: world-aquatics-rules §SW 8.1, §SW 8.2, §SW 8.3, §SW 8.4, §SW 8.5; usa-swimming §101.3; usms §101.3.3

- ○ **SW-3.5.A** Call legal or illegal: a described fly stroke with alternating kick, a breaststroke kick, or arms recovered under water.
  - EK: SW 8.2: both arms forward together over the water, back together under; SW 8.3: leg movements simultaneous, no breaststroke kick (FINA, USA Swimming).
  - P3
- ○ **SW-3.5.B** Call legal or illegal for a Masters butterfly using breaststroke kick, and state how USMS differs from World Aquatics.
  - EK: USMS 101.3.3: breaststroke kick may be used exclusively or interchangeably with dolphin kick, one per arm pull except before turn and finish; illegal under SW 8.3.
  - P3
- ○ **SW-3.5.C** Call legal or illegal: a described fly turn or finish touch (one hand, stacked hands, hands under water, staggered touch).
  - EK: SW 8.4: both hands, separated and simultaneous, at, above or below the surface; 'separated' means not stacked — no visible gap needed.
  - P3
- ○ **SW-3.5.D** Call legal or illegal: body position during fly underwaters (on the side, on the back before the first pull).
  - EK: SW 8.1: from the first arm stroke after start or turn the body stays on the breast; underwater kicking on the side is allowed.
  - P3

## Unit 4 · Breaststroke and individual medley

Big ideas: PUSH, RHYTHM, RULES

### 4.1 Breaststroke technique

Book: asca §L2 Stroke School; sanders-lift §breaststroke exception

- ○ **SW-4.1.A** Describe breaststroke timing (pull, breathe, kick, glide) and explain why it is the most drag-sensitive stroke.
  - EK: The recovery of arms and legs happens under water against the flow; the stroke's speed swings most within a cycle, so a streamlined glide carries the cycle.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-4.1.B** Diagnose a breaststroke fault (knees drawn under the hips, late kick, wide pull past the shoulders) and choose the correction.
  - EK: Heels to seat with knees narrow cuts frontal area; kick as the hands shoot forward.
  - P5

### 4.2 Breaststroke and medley rules

Book: world-aquatics-rules §SW 7.1, §SW 7.2, §SW 7.4, §SW 7.5, §SW 7.6, §SW 9.1, §SW 9.3, §SW 9.4; usa-swimming §101.2

- ○ **SW-4.2.A** Call legal or illegal: a described breaststroke pull-out (number of dolphin kicks, when the head surfaces).
  - EK: SW 7.1: one butterfly kick allowed before the first breaststroke kick; head must break the surface before the hands turn inward at the widest part of the second stroke.
  - P3
- ○ **SW-4.2.B** Call legal or illegal: a described breaststroke cycle, kick or touch.
  - EK: SW 7.2: one arm stroke then one leg kick; SW 7.4–7.5 simultaneous legs, no downward fly kick; SW 7.6 two hands, separated, simultaneous.
  - P3
- ○ **SW-4.2.C** Call legal or illegal: a described IM or medley-relay transition (order, stroke on the free leg, touch at each change).
  - EK: SW 9.1 IM: fly, back, breast, free; SW 9.3 medley relay: back, breast, fly, free; SW 9.4 each section finishes by its own stroke's rule; the free leg of IM can't be swum on the back.
  - P3

### 4.3 Racing the IM

Book: asca §L2 Stroke School

- ○ **SW-4.3.A** Compute the split share of each stroke in a 200 or 400 IM and identify the weak leg against a reference swimmer.
  - EK: Share_i = t_i / Σt; compare leg differentials, not totals.
  - P4 · P6

## Unit 5 · Starts, turns and underwaters

Big ideas: DRAG, RULES, PUSH

### 5.1 Starts

Book: world-aquatics-rules §SW 4.1, §SW 4.2, §SW 4.4; usms §101.1.1; asca §L2 Stroke School

- ○ **SW-5.1.A** Break a start into reaction, block, flight and entry phases and say what each contributes to 15 m time.
  - EK: Reaction ≈ 0.6–0.7 s for elite; the race is won in the push impulse and a clean, single-hole entry that keeps speed.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-5.1.B** Explain how entry angle and depth trade wave drag against lost speed and time to surface.
  - EK: Too flat ⇒ wave drag and splash; too deep ⇒ longer path back up; the target is a depth below most wave drag with a short, steady breakout.
  - Needs: SW-1.1.C
  - P2 · P6
- ○ **SW-5.1.C** Call legal or illegal: a described start (moving before the signal, false start after the signal, Masters in-water start).
  - EK: SW 4.4: starting before the signal is a DQ, judged when the signal has sounded; USMS 101.1.1 allows in-water starts facing any direction.
  - P3

### 5.2 Turns

Book: world-aquatics-rules §SW 5.2, §SW 6.4, §SW 7.6, §SW 8.4; asca §L2 Stroke School

- ○ **SW-5.2.A** Describe an open (two-hand) turn for fly and breast and explain how to shorten wall time.
  - EK: Touch, drop one elbow, tuck knees tight, throw the head and arm over; a small rotation radius turns faster (angular momentum).
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-5.2.B** Compute wall time (in-time + out-time) from 5 m in and 5 m out splits and compare two turns.
  - EK: Turn time = time from 5 m before the wall to 5 m after; compare at equal distances.
  - P4
- ○ **SW-5.2.C** Call legal or illegal: a described backstroke flip turn (shoulders past vertical, extra kicks or glides on the front, pushing off on the stomach).
  - EK: SW 6.4: after leaving the back, a continuous single or double arm pull starts the turn; touch the wall; be back on the back when leaving the wall.
  - P3

### 5.3 Underwaters and the 15 m rule

Book: world-aquatics-rules §SW 5.3, §SW 6.3, §SW 8.5; usa-swimming §101.3.2, §101.4.2

- ○ **SW-5.3.A** Call legal or illegal: a described fly, back or free breakout relative to the 15 m mark.
  - EK: SW 5.3, 6.3, 8.5: completely submerged ≤ 15 m after start and each turn; the head must break the surface by 15 m; one arm pull under water is permitted in fly (SW 8.5).
  - P3
- ○ **SW-5.3.B** Compute whether an underwater segment is faster than swimming the same distance, from underwater and surface velocities.
  - EK: Δt = d/v_swim − d/v_UW; stay under while v_UW > v_swim and the kick is sustainable.
  - Needs: SW-7.1.A
  - P4 · P6
- ○ **SW-5.3.C** Choose the number of underwater dolphin kicks for each wall of a 100 and 200 fly and justify it.
  - EK: Underwaters cost oxygen; holding the same count on every wall needs a count you can repeat when tired.
  - P6
- ○ **SW-5.3.D** Explain the breakout: why the first stroke should begin just before surfacing.
  - EK: Rising at kicking speed and stroking as the head breaks keeps velocity; surfacing then pausing loses the underwater speed.
  - P2

## Unit 6 · Rules, officiating and disqualifications

Big ideas: RULES

### 6.1 Race rules and relays

Book: world-aquatics-rules §SW 10.2, §SW 10.4, §SW 10.7, §SW 10.9, §SW 10.11, §SW 10.12, §SW 10.13, §SW 10.18; usa-swimming §101.7; usms §101.7

- ○ **SW-6.1.A** Call legal or illegal: a described race incident (pulling on the lane rope, changing lanes, standing on the bottom in free, pace device).
  - EK: SW 10.4 same lane; SW 10.7 no pulling on the lane rope; SW 10.9 no aids or tape; SW 10.18 no pace-making device.
  - P3
- ○ **SW-6.1.B** Call legal or illegal: a described relay exchange from touch time and the next swimmer's feet-off time.
  - EK: SW 10.11 / USA 101.7.3F: feet must stay on the platform until the incoming swimmer touches; exchange = feet-off − touch, negative = early. Electronic platforms are confirmed by human judges.
  - P3 · P4
- ○ **SW-6.1.C** Compute a relay leg's split and its reaction-time gain over a flat start from a result sheet.
  - EK: Leg split = cumulative time at its touch − previous touch; relay legs run fast because the exchange (~0.2–0.3 s) replaces a reaction to the gun.
  - P4

### 6.2 How officiating works

- ○ **SW-6.2.A** Explain who can disqualify, what they must see, and why the benefit of the doubt goes to the swimmer.
  - EK: Stroke and turn judges report what they observe in their jurisdiction; the referee decides; an infraction must be seen, not inferred.

## Unit 7 · Race analysis and pacing

Big ideas: RHYTHM, LOOP, ENERGY

### 7.1 Velocity, pace and course conversion

Book: toussaint-2006 §velocity per lap

- ○ **SW-7.1.A** Compute average velocity (m/s) and pace per 100 from a distance and time.
  - EK: v = d/t; pace/100 = 100·t/d; 1 yd = 0.9144 m.
  - P4
- ○ **SW-7.1.B** Compute splits and split differentials from cumulative times and classify a swim as even, positive or negative split.
  - EK: Split = cumulative_i − cumulative_(i−1); negative split ⇒ second half faster than the first.
  - P4
- ○ **SW-7.1.C** Compute clean-swimming velocity for a 25 or 50 by removing the 15 m off the wall and the 5 m in.
  - EK: Clean velocity = mid-pool distance / mid-pool time; it isolates swimming from walls.
  - Needs: SW-7.1.A
  - P4

### 7.2 Stroke rate, distance per stroke and stroke index

Book: toussaint-2006 §SR vs SL in fatigue

- ○ **SW-7.2.A** Compute stroke rate (cycles/min), distance per cycle and velocity from any two of them, or from a cycle count and lap time.
  - EK: v = SR·DPS with SR in cycles/s; SR (cyc/min) = 60 / cycle time; DPS = distance swum / cycles.
  - P4
- ○ **SW-7.2.B** Explain from data whether a swimmer slowed in a race by losing stroke rate or distance per stroke.
  - EK: Toussaint 2006: in a 100 m sprint, velocity fell 12% while SR fell 11% — slowing tracked SR, not SL.
  - Needs: SW-7.2.A
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **SW-7.2.C** Compute stroke index (v × DPS) and compare the efficiency of two swims at different speeds.
  - EK: SI = v·DPS (m²/s); higher SI at the same speed means more distance per cycle.
  - P4
- ○ **SW-7.2.D** Predict the new velocity when SR or DPS changes by a given percentage, and the trade-off needed to hold pace.
  - EK: (1 + Δv) = (1 + ΔSR)(1 + ΔDPS); +5% SR with −5% DPS ≈ −0.25% speed.
  - P4 · P6

### 7.3 Pacing strategy

Book: maglischo-1997 §energy metabolism

- ○ **SW-7.3.A** Choose a pacing profile for a 100 and 200 fly and justify it from energy supply and drag.
  - EK: Going out too fast spends glycolytic capacity early and drives acidosis; since power ∝ v³, uneven pace costs more energy than even pace for the same average speed.
  - P2 · P6
- ○ **SW-7.3.B** Build target splits for a goal time with a chosen front-half/back-half differential.
  - EK: Split targets sum to the goal; first 50 includes the dive (~1.5–2.5 s faster than later 50s).
  - Needs: SW-7.1.B
  - P4 · P6
- ○ **SW-7.3.C** Diagnose a pacing error from a split sheet (dying third 50, flat middle, too-easy start) and prescribe the change.
  - EK: Compare each split to the plan; the largest drop-off shows where effort or technique broke.
  - P5

## Unit 8 · Energy systems and training physiology

Big ideas: ENERGY

### 8.1 The three energy systems

Book: maglischo-1997 §energy metabolism summarized; toussaint-1994 §aerobic and anaerobic supply

- ○ **SW-8.1.A** Match each energy system to its fuel, rate, capacity and time course.
  - EK: ATP-CP: fastest, ~10–15 s; anaerobic glycolysis: fast, makes lactate and H⁺, limits after ~30–90 s; aerobic: slowest rate, largest capacity, carbohydrate then fat.
  - P2
- ○ **SW-8.1.B** Estimate the aerobic/anaerobic mix of a race from its duration.
  - EK: Contributions cross near ~75 s of all-out effort; a 50 is mostly anaerobic, a 200 mostly aerobic, a 100 fly near even.
  - Needs: SW-8.1.A
  - P2 · P6

### 8.2 Thresholds and adaptation

Book: maglischo-1997 §endurance training; css §Wakayoshi 1993 MLSS

- ○ **SW-8.2.A** Define lactate threshold and maximal lactate steady state and explain why they anchor training paces.
  - EK: The highest pace where lactate production and removal balance; above it, lactate climbs and time to exhaustion shortens sharply.
  - P2
- ○ **SW-8.2.B** Match a training stimulus to the adaptation it produces (capillarity, mitochondria, buffering, power).
  - EK: Basic and threshold work build aerobic capacity; lactate tolerance builds buffering; short maximal sprints build power and rate of glycolysis.
  - P2

## Unit 9 · Training science — CSS, intervals, periodization and taper

Big ideas: ENERGY, LOOP

### 9.1 Critical swim speed

Book: css §Wakayoshi 1992, §Ginn 1993, §% CSS zones

- ○ **SW-9.1.A** Compute CSS (m/s and pace per 100) from 400 and 200 time-trial times.
  - EK: CSS = (400 − 200)/(T₄₀₀ − T₂₀₀) m/s; pace/100 = (T₄₀₀ − T₂₀₀)/2 s. Variant: 350/(T₄₀₀ − T₅₀).
  - P4
- ○ **SW-9.1.B** Compute target paces per 100 for a training zone given CSS and a %CSS.
  - EK: Pace/100 = 100 / (f·CSS); zones roughly 70% recovery, 80–87% endurance, ~95% threshold, 100–110% VO₂max.
  - Needs: SW-9.1.A
  - P4
- ○ **SW-9.1.C** Explain what CSS approximates and its limits, including for butterfly.
  - EK: CSS approximates MLSS within a few %; it overestimates for poorly paced trials and is stroke-specific — test fly separately.
  - P2

### 9.2 Interval training

Book: maglischo-1997 §basic, §threshold, §overload, §lactate tolerance, §lactate production, §power

- ○ **SW-9.2.A** Compute rest per repeat, work:rest ratio and total set time from a set's distance, send-off and swim times.
  - EK: Rest = send-off − swim time; set time = repeats × send-off; W:R = swim/rest.
  - P4
- ○ **SW-9.2.B** Classify a described set (e.g. 10×100 @ threshold on :15 rest; 6×50 max on 3:00) into its training category.
  - EK: Maglischo: basic (≥1000, 4–5 s/100 slower than threshold, :05–:30 rest); threshold (2000–3000, :10–:30); lactate tolerance (75–200 repeats, 3–7 min rest, 600–1000 total); lactate production (25–50, 1–3 min); power (5–25, 100–300 total).
  - P3 · P6
- ○ **SW-9.2.C** Explain why rest interval, not distance, mostly sets which system a repeat trains.
  - EK: Short rest keeps the aerobic system loaded; long rest restores phosphagens and lets each repeat go glycolytic or maximal.
  - P2

### 9.3 Periodization and load

Book: asca §L3 Season Planning Framework; maglischo-1997 §balancing training

- ○ **SW-9.3.A** Lay out a season into macro-, meso- and microcycles around a target meet.
  - EK: General prep → specific prep → competition → taper; volume peaks before intensity peaks.
  - P6
- ○ **SW-9.3.B** Compute a week's volume by training category and compare it with a recommended balance.
  - EK: Maglischo: warm-up/swim-down 15–20%, threshold + overload 20–25%, basic 40–50%, sprint 5–10%.
  - P4 · P6

### 9.4 Taper

Book: bosquet-2007 §meta-analysis of 27 studies

- ○ **SW-9.4.A** Design a taper by the evidence: duration, volume cut, intensity and frequency.
  - EK: Bosquet 2007: ~2 weeks, cut volume 41–60% exponentially, keep intensity and session frequency.
  - P6
- ○ **SW-9.4.B** Compute daily yardage for an exponential taper from starting volume and target reduction.
  - EK: V(t) = V₀·e^(−t/τ); choose τ so V(end) = (1 − cut)·V₀.
  - Needs: SW-9.4.A
  - P4

## Unit 10 · Self-analysis and video critique

Big ideas: LOOP, RHYTHM

### 10.1 Getting numbers from video

Book: asca §L2 Stroke School

- ○ **SW-10.1.A** Compute cycle time and stroke rate from a frame count and frame rate.
  - EK: Cycle time = frames / fps; SR (cyc/min) = 60·fps / frames.
  - Needs: SW-7.2.A
  - P4
- ○ **SW-10.1.B** Compute the time and distance resolution of a camera at a given swim speed and judge whether it can resolve a phase.
  - EK: Δt = 1/fps; Δx = v/fps — at 2 m/s, 30 fps ≈ 6.7 cm per frame, 120 fps ≈ 1.7 cm.
  - P4 · P5
- ○ **SW-10.1.C** Choose camera angles (side underwater, front, overhead) to see a given fault, and say what parallax hides.
  - EK: Side view shows timing and depth; front view shows symmetry and hand path; off-axis views distort angles.
  - P1 · P6

### 10.2 Critiquing your own stroke

Book: usms-fly §faults and corrections

- ○ **SW-10.2.A** Mark the key events (entry, catch, push, exit, kick 1, kick 2, breath) on a fly clip and measure their timing.
  - EK: Express events as % of cycle time so clips at different speeds compare.
  - Needs: SW-3.2.A
  - P1 · P4
- ○ **SW-10.2.B** Diagnose a fault from a described clip, rank it by cost, and choose one correction to work on first.
  - EK: Fix the fault that costs the most net force first, usually body position or timing before hand path.
  - P5
- ○ **SW-10.2.C** Design a before/after test (stroke count, SR, 25 time at fixed effort) that shows whether a correction worked.
  - EK: Hold effort or SR constant and measure the other; one change at a time; repeat trials to beat noise.
  - P5 · P6

## Unit 11 · Practice design

Big ideas: LOOP, ENERGY

### 11.1 Building a session

Book: asca §L3 Daily Workout Planning and Design; maglischo-1997 §balancing training

- ○ **SW-11.1.A** Write a session with warm-up, pre-set, main set and cool-down, each with a stated purpose.
  - EK: Warm-up raises muscle temperature and rehearses technique; the main set carries the day's training category; cool-down clears metabolites.
  - P6
- ○ **SW-11.1.B** Compute a written workout's total yardage and duration from its sets and send-offs.
  - EK: Total = Σ repeats × distance; duration = Σ repeats × send-off + set rests.
  - Needs: SW-9.2.A
  - P4
- ○ **SW-11.1.C** Choose send-offs for a set from a CSS or best-time pace and the intended rest.
  - EK: Send-off = target swim time + rest, rounded to a clock-friendly :05.
  - Needs: SW-9.1.B
  - P4 · P6

### 11.2 Technique work inside training

Book: asca §L2 Stroke School

- ○ **SW-11.2.A** Choose a drill that isolates a given fault (one-arm fly, 3-3-3, kick on side, head-lead body dolphin) and say what it trains and what it can't.
  - EK: A drill isolates one element; transfer needs a bridge back to full stroke at race tempo.
  - P5 · P6
- ○ **SW-11.2.B** Design a set that trains a stroke-count or SR target (e.g. hold count while descending time).
  - EK: Fix one of SR or DPS and progress the other; v = SR·DPS tells you which has to give.
  - Needs: SW-7.2.D
  - P6

### 11.3 Fitting training to a Masters / club life

Book: usms §Masters; asca §L3 Season Planning Framework

- ○ **SW-11.3.A** Adapt a week of 3+ mile days to class and work load so that the hard sessions stay hard and the easy ones stay easy.
  - EK: Polarised weeks (most volume easy, a few true hard sessions) beat uniform moderate effort for both performance and recovery.
  - P6

## Unit 12 · Coaching others (extension)

### 12.1 Teaching progressions

- ○ **SW-12.1.A** Sequence a butterfly teaching progression from body dolphin to full stroke and say what each step must show before moving on.
