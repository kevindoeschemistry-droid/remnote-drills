## Big ideas

- **ADV · Offense creates an advantage and keeps it; defense denies it and recovers.** Every possession is a fight over advantage — a step, a numbers edge, a mismatch. Good offense creates one and passes it on until it becomes a good shot; good defense stops the first one and rotates to cancel the next.
- **SPACE · Space and time are the resources.** Spacing sets how far each helper must travel; the clock sets how long anyone has. Reads are judgments about distance and time — who is a step late, who has to help, how many seconds are left.
- **POSS · The possession is the unit.** Teams get about the same number of possessions, so points per possession, not points, measures play. Four things decide a possession — shooting, turnovers, rebounding, free throws.
- **EV · Decisions are expected-value bets.** A shot, a foul, a timeout or a coverage is a bet with a probability and a payoff. The right choice maximises expected points, or win probability late, given the players actually on the floor.
- **RULES · The rules shape the strategy.** Shot clock, team-foul limits, timeout and throw-in rules differ by level (FIBA, NCAA, NFHS), and late-game strategy changes with them. Knowing the rule is part of knowing the play.
- **DEV · Decision-makers are developed, not drilled.** Game IQ grows from representative reps — reading live defenders under time pressure — sequenced by mastery, with competition earning a larger share as players advance.

## Practices

- **P1 · Read the floor.** Identify the defense, coverage, matchup, where the advantage is and who is the help — from a described possession, a diagram or film.
- **P2 · Decide.** Choose the action (shoot, pass, drive, cut; which coverage; foul or defend) and who it goes to, and say why it beats the alternatives.
- **P3 · Apply the rules.** State the rule for the level being played and what it allows or forbids in the situation — clock, fouls, timeouts, violations, throw-ins.
- **P4 · Compute.** Calculate possessions, efficiency, shooting percentages, expected value and clock math, with the right formula variant and units (per possession, per 100, per shot).
- **P5 · Evaluate with context.** Judge a number or a clip in context — sample size, opponent, role, selection bias, league and era — before drawing a conclusion.
- **P6 · Teach and design.** Turn a principle into a scouting key, a cue, a drill or a practice plan that transfers to games.

## Unit 1 · Offensive principles

Big ideas: ADV, SPACE

### 1.1 Spacing and floor balance

Book: usab-pdc §Introductory: floor and player awareness

- ○ **BB-1.1.A** Identify spacing errors in a described or diagrammed half-court set (two players in one help zone, empty corner, crowded lane) and name the fix.
  - EK: Spacing sets help distance; teaching starts at ~12 ft apart (a lane width) and widens to 15–18 ft and the three-point line as range grows.
  - EK: A filled corner forces the low helper to choose between the rim and a corner three.
  - P1
- ○ **BB-1.1.B** Given a 5-out, 4-out-1-in or 3-out-2-in alignment and a drive, choose which defender must help and which offensive player is left open.
  - EK: The nearest helper to the drive's path helps; whoever he left, and the rotation behind him, is the open man.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **BB-1.1.C** Choose the correct spacing move for each off-ball player on a drive — fill, lift, drift, or dive.
  - EK: Baseline drive: corner lifts to the wing or drifts behind; middle drive: wings drift to the corner, opposite shooters stay one pass away, the big dunker-spots.
  - P2

### 1.2 Creating an advantage

Book: usab-pdc §Introductory: triple threat

- ○ **BB-1.2.A** Name the ways an offense creates an advantage (ball screen, post touch, isolation, off-ball screen, transition, offensive rebound) and pick which fits a given personnel group.
  - EK: Advantage = a defender out of position or a numbers edge; it is created on-ball, off-ball or in the open floor.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **BB-1.2.B** Given a described catch, choose the triple-threat read: shoot if the closeout is late, drive the high foot if the defender is close, pass if help has arrived.
  - EK: The defender's distance and feet decide: space ⇒ shot, momentum toward you ⇒ drive past it, help shown ⇒ move it.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **BB-1.2.C** Recognise a mismatch in a described lineup (size, speed, a poor defender) and choose how to attack it — post, isolate, or force it into the action.
  - EK: Attack the mismatch quickly, before the defense scrambles back; involve the weak defender as screener or screened.
  - P1 · P2

### 1.3 Keeping the advantage — the advantage chain



- ○ **BB-1.3.A** Given a described drive-and-kick sequence, choose the next action for the receiver (shoot, attack the closeout, swing, or extra pass).
  - EK: Decide on the catch (~0.5 s): an advantage held too long dies as the defense recovers.
  - EK: The extra pass beats a two-on-one closeout; driving a hard closeout beats a scrambled rotation.
  - Needs: BB-1.2.B
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **BB-1.3.B** Distinguish a good shot from a quick shot in a described possession by naming the advantage it came from.
  - EK: A good shot is an open, in-range attempt from a created advantage; a contested early shot with no advantage wastes the possession.
  - P1 · P5

### 1.4 Off-ball movement and cutting

Book: usab-pdc §Introductory: move to meet the pass

- ○ **BB-1.4.A** Given how a defender plays an off-ball screen (trails, goes under, cheats over, top-locks), choose the cut: curl, pop/fade, flare, or backdoor.
  - EK: Trail ⇒ curl; under or sagging ⇒ pop or fade; top-locked or overplayed ⇒ backdoor; defender shoots the gap ⇒ flare or re-screen.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **BB-1.4.B** Choose when to backdoor, basket-cut or relocate when a defender denies or turns his head.
  - EK: Denial or ball-watching opens the backdoor; a cut must create a passing lane, not fill one already used.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **BB-1.4.C** Read a described post-entry: choose the entry angle and the post player's read against a double from the top, the baseline or no double.
  - EK: Enter from the wing at a 45° angle; a double from the top leaves the passer; from the baseline it leaves the opposite block or corner.

### 1.5 Transition offense



- ○ **BB-1.5.A** Assign transition lanes and roles (ball, wings sprinting to corners, rim-runner, trailer) for a given rebound or turnover.
  - EK: Wide lanes stretch the retreat; the rim-runner forces the deepest defender to pick him up; the trailer arrives for a late three or a drag screen.
  - P2 · P6
- ○ **BB-1.5.B** Given a 2-on-1, 3-on-2 or 4-on-3 break, choose who shoots or receives the pass.

## Unit 3 · Defensive principles

Big ideas: ADV, SPACE

### 3.1 Ball and off-ball positioning

Book: usab-pdc §Introductory: team defensive concepts

- ○ **BB-3.1.A** Place each defender correctly given where the ball is — on-ball, one pass away (deny or gap), two passes away (help side).
  - EK: Ball–you–man: see both; one pass away up the line, two passes away on the line (in the paint), nail help on middle drives.
  - EK: On-ball: between the man and the basket, about an arm's length, eyes on the waist.
  - P1
- ○ **BB-3.1.B** Choose the direction to force the ball (baseline, middle, weak hand) for a given scheme and scouting report, and justify it.
  - EK: Forcing baseline needs a low helper waiting; forcing middle relies on bodies in the gaps. Either way, take away the strength.
  - P2

### 3.2 Help and rotation

Book: usab-pdc §help-side deferred past Introductory

- ○ **BB-3.2.A** Given a described drive or post touch, name who helps, who rotates to the helper's man, and who is left (the X-out or sink-and-fill).
  - EK: Help comes from the closest gap; rotation goes toward the basket first; the last defender splits two shooters.
  - P1 · P2
- ○ **BB-3.2.B** Identify the low man on a described possession and his job when the ball is driven or a roller dives.
  - EK: Low man = deepest weak-side defender; he protects the rim or tags the roller, and the help behind him zones the two weak-side shooters.
  - P1
- ○ **BB-3.2.C** Choose whether to stunt, dig or fully help on a drive given the driver and the shooter being left.
  - EK: Stunt and recover vs a non-shooter's teammate; never fully help off an elite shooter one pass away.
  - P2 · P5

### 3.3 Closeouts



- ○ **BB-3.3.A** Choose the closeout type (high-hands shot contest, short closeout to take away the drive, run-by) for a described shooter and game situation.
  - EK: Sprint, then chop the last steps with hands high; close out short on a driver, high on a shooter; late in the game vs a must-make three, run him off the line.
  - P1 · P2

### 3.4 Defensive rebounding and transition defense



- ○ **BB-3.4.A** Assign box-out responsibilities on a described shot, including the shooter and the weak side, where misses go long.
  - EK: Hit first, then pursue; long shots bounce long, often to the weak side.
  - P2
- ○ **BB-3.4.B** Given a described live-ball turnover or long rebound, choose the transition defense priorities in order: stop the ball, protect the rim, find shooters, match up.
  - EK: First back takes the rim, the next stops the ball; matching up comes after the paint and the ball are safe.
  - P2

## Unit 6 · Situational basketball

Big ideas: EV, RULES

### 6.1 Clock management

Book: fiba-obr §Art. 29; ncaa §2-11.6 shot-clock reset

- ○ **BB-6.1.A** Given the game clock, shot clock length and score, compute whether a 2-for-1 is available and when to shoot.
  - EK: Shoot when game clock ≈ shot clock + 6–10 s: the opponent can't run out the clock, and you still get the last possession.
  - P3 · P4
- ○ **BB-6.1.B** Given score, game clock and shot clock, decide whether the leading team can run the clock out, and when the trailing team must foul.
  - EK: If the game clock is below the shot clock, the leader with the ball can drain it unless fouled; trailing by more than one possession late means foul to stop the clock.
  - P2 · P3 · P4
- ○ **BB-6.1.C** Choose when to start the attack for a last shot of a quarter or game (hold for one, leave time for a rebound).
  - EK: Start the action at ~8–6 s so a miss leaves no time for the opponent but a tip remains possible.
  - P2 · P4

### 6.2 End-of-game shot and foul decisions

Book: kenpom §fouling when up 3; nfhs §4-8-1; ncaa §team-foul bonus; fiba-obr §Art. 41

- ○ **BB-6.2.A** Given a lead of 3, seconds left and the opponent's shooters, decide whether to foul or defend, citing the evidence.
  - EK: kenpom's college sample: defend ≈ 93.5% vs foul ≈ 92.0% wins, noisy and selection-biased. Fouling is worth it mainly against several good shooters and with confidence in the defensive rebound.
  - EK: A foul in the act of a three, or a missed FT the trailing team rebounds, is the main risk.
  - P2 · P5
- ○ **BB-6.2.B** Trailing by 2 with one possession left, compute the win probability of going for 3 vs going for 2 and overtime, and choose.
  - EK: P(win | go for 3) = P(make 3); P(win | go for 2) = P(make 2) × P(win OT) — OT is roughly a coin flip unless one side is clearly better.
  - P4 · P2
- ○ **BB-6.2.C** Trailing late, choose whom to foul and compute the expected points conceded given the bonus rule for the level.
  - EK: Two-shot foul ⇒ expected points = 2 × FT%; under a one-and-one ⇒ p + p² (NCAA men's 7th–9th foul).
  - EK: NFHS: two shots from the 5th team foul in a quarter; FIBA: two shots after 4 team fouls in a quarter; NCAA women: two from the 5th per quarter.
  - P3 · P4
- ○ **BB-6.2.D** Choose whether to use a foul to give when leading late, given the opponent's team fouls and time left.
  - EK: Below the bonus, a non-shooting foul costs only a throw-in and seconds, and breaks up a set play.
  - P2 · P3

- ○ **BB-6.4.A** Choose a baseline or sideline out-of-bounds set for a described need (quick two, a three, a safe inbound) and identify the primary and the safety.

## Unit 7 · Rules and officiating

### 7.1 Clocks and violations

Book: fiba-obr §Art. 26, §Art. 27, §Art. 28, §Art. 29; ncaa §2-11.6

- ○ **BB-7.1.A** Given a described sequence, state the shot-clock reset for the level (FIBA 24 / 14; NCAA men 30 / 20 off the rim in the frontcourt).
  - EK: FIBA: frontcourt throw-in after a stoppage resets to 14 if 13 or fewer remain. NCAA men: ball hits the rim and the offense keeps it in the frontcourt ⇒ 20; in the backcourt ⇒ 30.
  - P3
- ○ **BB-7.1.B** Identify the violation in a described play — 3 s, 5 s closely guarded, 5 s throw-in, 8 s backcourt, backcourt, travel — and its penalty.
  - EK: FIBA: 3 s in the restricted area; 5 s closely guarded or to release a throw-in; 8 s to cross half-court.

### 7.2 Fouls and their penalties

Book: fiba-obr §Art. 34, §Art. 37, §Art. 41; nfhs §4-8-1; ncaa §team-foul bonus, §10-1 flagrant

- ○ **BB-7.2.A** Given a level and a team-foul count, state the free throws awarded on a non-shooting foul.
  - EK: NFHS: two shots from the 5th team foul per quarter, reset each quarter. NCAA men: 1-and-1 from the 7th, two from the 10th per half. NCAA women: two from the 5th per quarter. FIBA: two after 4 per quarter; OT fouls count to the 4th quarter.

- ○ **BB-7.2.B** State the free throws for a shooting foul (made two, missed two, missed three) and for an unsportsmanlike foul.

- ○ **BB-7.2.C** Judge whether a described late-game foul is a common foul or unsportsmanlike under FIBA criteria.
  - EK: Unsportsmanlike: no legitimate attempt to play the ball, excessive contact, stopping a fast break, or contact from behind with no defender between the player and the basket.
  - P3 · P1

- ○ **BB-7.3.A** Call a described block/charge play using the legal-guarding-position standard.

  - P3 · P1
- ○ **BB-7.3.B** Call goaltending or basket interference in a described play and state the result.

  - P3

## Unit 8 · Analytics

Big ideas: POSS, EV

### 8.1 Possessions and efficiency

Book: bbref §TOV%; kenpom §four factors; ts §0.44

- ○ **BB-8.1.A** Compute a team's possessions from a box score.
  - EK: Poss ≈ FGA − ORB + TOV + 0.44·FTA; average the two teams' estimates for one game.
  - P4
- ○ **BB-8.1.B** Compute points per possession, offensive and defensive rating (per 100), and net rating from a box score.
  - EK: ORtg = 100·PTS/Poss; DRtg = 100·Opp PTS/Poss; net = ORtg − DRtg. Pace = possessions per 40 or 48 minutes.
  - Needs: BB-8.1.A
  - P4
- ○ **BB-8.1.C** Explain why per-possession numbers beat per-game numbers when comparing a fast team and a slow team, using a worked comparison.
  - EK: Points per game mixes pace with efficiency; per-100 isolates efficiency.
  - Needs: BB-8.1.B
  - P5

### 8.2 Shooting efficiency

Book: bbref §shooting; ts §formula

- ○ **BB-8.2.A** Compute eFG% from FGM, 3PM and FGA, and explain why it exceeds FG% for three-point shooters.
  - EK: eFG% = (FGM + 0.5·3PM)/FGA: a three counts as 1.5 makes.
  - P4
- ○ **BB-8.2.B** Compute TS% and true shot attempts from PTS, FGA and FTA.
  - EK: TS% = PTS / (2·(FGA + 0.44·FTA)); 0.44 reflects the mix of 2-shot, 3-shot and and-one trips (0.5 if all were 2-shot) and differs by league.
  - P4
- ○ **BB-8.2.C** Compare two players' efficiency with FG%, eFG% and TS%, and say which measure fits the question.
  - EK: FG% ignores threes and FTs; eFG% adds threes; TS% adds free throws.
  - Needs: BB-8.2.A, BB-8.2.B
  - P4 · P5

### 8.3 The four factors

Book: bbref §four factors; kenpom §four factors

- ○ **BB-8.3.A** Compute all four factors for both teams from a box score.
  - EK: eFG%; TOV% = TOV/(FGA + 0.44·FTA + TOV); ORB% = ORB/(ORB + Opp DRB); FT rate = FT/FGA (bbref) or FTA/FGA (kenpom).
  - Needs: BB-8.2.A
  - P4
- ○ **BB-8.3.B** Given two teams' four factors, identify which factor decided the game and weigh it.
  - EK: Oliver's approximate weights: shooting 40%, turnovers 25%, rebounding 20%, free throws 15%.
  - Needs: BB-8.3.A
  - P4 · P5
- ○ **BB-8.3.C** Translate a four-factors deficit into a coaching emphasis (shot selection, ball security, crashing, attacking the rim).
  - EK: Each factor maps to a controllable habit; fix the largest weighted gap first.
  - Needs: BB-8.3.B
  - P6 · P5

### 8.4 Expected value of shots and decisions



- ○ **BB-8.4.A** Compute the expected points of a shot from its make probability and value, and compare shot types (corner three, above-the-break three, mid-range, rim).
  - EK: EV = p × points: 36% from three ≈ 1.08; 50% at two = 1.00; 40% mid-range = 0.80.
  - EK: Shooting fouls add value: fold in P(foul) × expected FT points.
  - P4
- ○ **BB-8.4.B** Compute the break-even make percentage at which a three equals a given two, and use it to judge a shot choice.
  - EK: Break-even p₃ = (2·p₂)/3; a 50% two equals a 33.3% three.
  - Needs: BB-8.4.A
  - P4 · P2
- ○ **BB-8.4.C** Include offensive rebounding in a shot's expected value and compare a quick shot with working for a better one.
  - EK: EV ≈ p·v + (1 − p)·ORB%·PPP_second-chance; passing up a shot costs some turnover risk and clock.
  - Needs: BB-8.4.A
  - P4 · P5

### 8.5 Reading numbers responsibly

Book: kenpom §fouling when up 3: caveats

- ○ **BB-8.5.A** Judge whether a described stat (a shooter's 3P%, a lineup's net rating, a late-game study) has enough sample to act on.
  - EK: Three-point percentage and small-lineup ratings are noisy over a few hundred attempts or possessions; late-game samples are tiny and coaches choose when to foul.
  - P5
- ○ **BB-8.5.B** Identify selection bias or role confounding in a described analytic claim (e.g. 'defensive stops are higher when X is on the floor').
  - EK: Who plays, against whom and when is chosen, not random; on/off numbers carry teammates and opponents with them.
  - P5

## Unit 10 · Practice design and player development (extension)

Big ideas: DEV

### 10.1 Long-term development and levels

Book: usab-pdc §levels, §LTAD stages, §training:competition ratios

- ○ **BB-10.1.A** Place a described player on the USA Basketball level (Introductory, Foundational, Advanced, Performance) by skill mastery, not age, and name the matching LTAD stage.
  - EK: Levels are by mastery; LTAD stages run Active Start (0–6), Fundamentals (6–9), Learning to Train (8–12), Training to Train (12–15), Training to Compete (14–17), Training to Win (17+).
  - P1 · P6
- ○ **BB-10.1.B** Set the training-to-competition balance and position policy for a level.
  - EK: Foundational 70:30; Advanced 60:40 shifting to 50:50; Performance 25:75. Foundational: teach position concepts but don't assign positions.
  - P6
- ○ **BB-10.1.C** Sequence team concepts by level: what to teach before 5-on-5, when help-side defense enters, when positions are assigned.
  - EK: Introductory: team principles only, no 5-on-5 yet, no on-ball defending; help-side comes later; positions may be assigned late in Advanced.
  - P6

### 10.2 Designing practice for decisions



- ○ **BB-10.2.A** Convert a principle (e.g. drive-and-kick reads) into a small-sided, competitive drill with live defenders and a scoring rule that rewards the right decision.
  - EK: Decisions transfer from representative reps: live defense, game spacing, time pressure; scoring rewards the read, not just the make.
  - P6
- ○ **BB-10.2.B** Build a practice plan with progression (1-on-1 → 2-on-2 → 3-on-3 → 5-on-5), time blocks and stated objectives for a given week.
  - EK: Add players to add reads; every block names the objective it serves.
  - P6
- ○ **BB-10.2.C** Write a short teaching cue for a skill or read and check that it names an observable action.
  - EK: Cues like B.E.E.F. (balance, elbow, eyes, follow-through) compress a model into words a player can act on mid-rep.
  - P6

### 10.3 Evaluating players and the program



- ○ **BB-10.3.A** Design an evaluation of a player's decision-making (tagged reads correct / total) separate from shot-making.
  - EK: A right read that misses is still a right read; grade decisions and outcomes separately.
  - P5 · P6
- ○ **BB-10.3.B** Set season-level team goals from the four factors and track them per game.
  - EK: Targets for each factor, on both ends, turn analytics into weekly practice priorities.
