## Unit 1 · Topological spaces

Big ideas: OPEN

### 1.1 Open sets and continuity



- ● **TP-1.1.A** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Check the axioms of a topology on a small set and generate a topology from given sets.
  - EK: A topology contains the empty set and the whole space and is closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
  - Needs: RA-4.1.A, TP-1.2.B
  - P2 · drills: tp-is-topology, tp-generated-topology
- ● **TP-1.1.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Find the closure and interior of a subset.
  - EK: The closure is the smallest closed set containing A (closed means the complement is open); the interior is the largest open set inside A.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.A, TP-1.2.C
  - P1 · drills: tp-closure-size, tp-interior-size
- ● **TP-1.1.C** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Recognise continuity, homeomorphism and topological properties, including the Hausdorff property.
  - EK: f is continuous when preimages of open sets are open. A homeomorphism is a continuous bijection with continuous inverse; properties it preserves are topological. In a Hausdorff space distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods, so limits are unique.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.B, TP-2.2.A
  - P2 · drills: tp-topological-property, tp-hausdorff-facts
- ● **TP-1.1.D** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1, cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S4) Describe subspace, product and quotient topologies and identify a quotient of an interval or square.
  - EK: A subspace takes the intersections of open sets with it; a product is generated by products of open sets; a quotient makes a set open when its preimage is. Gluing the ends of an interval gives a circle; gluing opposite sides of a square gives a torus.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.C, TP-2.2.B
  - P2 · drills: tp-quotient-identify, tp-product-discrete

### 1.2 Distances and open sets on the line



- ● **TP-1.2.A** (cambridge-ib-analysis-ii:M, cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Check the axioms of a metric and compute distances in different metrics.
  - EK: A metric is non-negative, zero only between equal points, symmetric, and satisfies the triangle inequality. The Euclidean, taxicab and maximum metrics on the plane give different distances but the same open sets.
  - Needs: RA-6.2.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-metric-distance, tp-is-metric
- ● **TP-1.2.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Decide whether a subset of the real line is open, closed, both or neither.
  - EK: A set is open if every point has a small interval around it inside the set, and closed if its complement is open. Only the empty set and the whole line are both; a half-open interval is neither.
  - Needs: TP-1.2.A, RA-4.1.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-open-closed-line, tp-open-closed-facts
- ● **TP-1.2.C** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Find the boundary, interior and closure of a union of intervals.
  - EK: The interior drops every endpoint, the closure adds every endpoint, and the boundary is the closure minus the interior: the endpoints and isolated points.
  - Needs: TP-1.2.B
  - P1 · drills: tp-boundary-count, tp-closure-interior-line
- ● **TP-1.2.D** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Identify the limit points of a set and relate them to closedness.
  - EK: x is a limit point of A if every neighbourhood of x meets A in a point other than x. A set is closed exactly when it contains all its limit points.
  - Needs: TP-1.2.C, RA-2.2.B
  - P1 · drills: tp-limit-point-count, tp-limit-point-facts

## Unit 2 · Connectedness

Big ideas: INV

### 2.1 Connected and path-connected spaces



- ● **TP-2.1.A** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S2) Decide whether a space is connected and count its components.
  - EK: A space is connected when it is not the union of two disjoint non-empty open sets. Removing a point disconnects the line but not the plane, which is one way to tell them apart.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.C, TP-3.2.B
  - P1 · drills: tp-components-line, tp-connected-or-not
- ● **TP-2.1.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S2) Relate connectedness, path-connectedness and continuous images.
  - EK: A continuous image of a connected space is connected (the intermediate value theorem is the case of an interval). Path-connected implies connected; the converse holds for open subsets of Euclidean space but fails for the topologist's sine curve.
  - Needs: TP-2.1.A, RA-4.1.B
  - P2 · drills: tp-connected-theorems, tp-homeomorphic-or-not

### 2.2 Continuity on small spaces



- ● **TP-2.2.A** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Test a map between finite spaces for continuity by pulling back open sets.
  - EK: f is continuous when the preimage of every open set is open. Into the two-point Sierpinski space, a continuous map is the same thing as a choice of open set.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-finite-continuous, tp-continuity-finite-facts
- ● **TP-2.2.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S1) Generate a topology from a basis or subbasis.
  - EK: A subbasis generates the topology whose open sets are unions of finite intersections of its members; it is the smallest topology in which those sets are open.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-subbasis-topology, tp-basis-facts

## Unit 3 · Compactness

Big ideas: INV, OPEN

### 3.1 Compact spaces



- ● **TP-3.1.A** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S3) Decide whether a space is compact.
  - EK: Compact means every open cover has a finite subcover. Heine-Borel: a subset of Euclidean space is compact exactly when it is closed and bounded.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.C, RA-2.1.B, TP-3.2.A, TP-1.2.D
  - P2 · drills: tp-compact-or-not, tp-cover-count
- ● **TP-3.1.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S3) Use what compactness guarantees.
  - EK: Closed subsets of compact spaces are compact; compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed; continuous images of compact spaces are compact; a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism.
  - Needs: TP-3.1.A, TP-2.1.B
  - P2 · drills: tp-compact-theorems, tp-cover-no-finite

### 3.2 Covers by hand



- ● **TP-3.2.A** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S3) Extract a smallest subcover from a cover.
  - EK: A subcover keeps some of the cover's sets and still covers the space. Compactness says a finite one always exists; finding the smallest is a counting problem.
  - Needs: TP-1.2.B
  - P1 · drills: tp-min-subcover, tp-unit-interval-cover
- ● **TP-3.2.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S2) Count the connected components of a graph or a union of intervals.
  - EK: Components are the maximal connected pieces. For a graph, V − E + (number of independent cycles) counts them; for intervals on a line, pieces that overlap or touch merge.
  - Needs: TP-1.2.B
  - P1 · drills: tp-graph-components, tp-interval-components

## Unit 4 · Surfaces

Big ideas: INV

### 4.1 Polygons, Euler characteristic and classification



- ● **TP-4.1.A** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S5) Compute an Euler characteristic and the genus it implies.
  - EK: χ = V − E + F for any triangulation or polygonal division. A closed orientable surface of genus g has χ = 2 − 2g: sphere 2, torus 0, double torus −2.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.D, TP-4.2.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-euler-characteristic, tp-genus-from-chi
- ● **TP-4.1.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S4) Read a polygon with sides identified: count its vertices and name the surface.
  - EK: Follow each corner round the gluing to see which corners become one point. With one face and n edge pairs, χ = V − n + 1. Standard words: a a⁻¹ sphere, a a projective plane, a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹ torus, a b a b⁻¹ Klein bottle.
  - Needs: TP-4.1.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-polygon-vertices, tp-polygon-surface
- ● **TP-4.1.C** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S5) Use orientability and the Euler characteristic to classify a closed surface.
  - EK: Every closed surface is a sphere with g handles (χ = 2 − 2g) or a sphere with k crosscaps (χ = 2 − k). Orientability and χ together determine the surface. A connected sum has χ(A # B) = χ(A) + χ(B) − 2.
  - Needs: TP-4.1.B, TP-4.2.C
  - P1 · drills: tp-chi-from-word, tp-connected-sum

### 4.2 Surfaces by Euler's formula



- ● **TP-4.2.A** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S5) Use Euler's formula on the sphere and on other surfaces.
  - EK: A division of a surface into faces satisfies V − E + F = χ: 2 on a sphere, 0 on a torus. So a map on a torus has two fewer faces than the same vertex and edge counts would give on a sphere.
  - Needs: TP-1.1.D
  - P1 · drills: tp-faces-on-surface, tp-euler-formula-facts
- ● **TP-4.2.B** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S5) Count handles or crosscaps from the Euler characteristic.
  - EK: An orientable closed surface has g = (2 − χ)/2 handles; a non-orientable one has k = 2 − χ crosscaps. One crosscap is the projective plane, two the Klein bottle.
  - Needs: TP-4.1.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-crosscaps, tp-sum-genus
- ● **TP-4.2.C** (cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S4, cambridge-ib-topological-spaces:S5) Name a closed surface from its orientability and Euler characteristic.
  - EK: The pair (orientable or not, χ) determines a closed surface: orientable χ = 2 sphere, 0 torus, −2 double torus; non-orientable χ = 1 projective plane, 0 Klein bottle.
  - Needs: TP-4.2.B
  - P1 · drills: tp-name-surface, tp-classification-facts

## Unit 5 · The fundamental group and homology (extension)

Big ideas: INV

### 5.1 Loops, coverings and holes



- ● **TP-5.1.A** (cambridge-ii-algebraic-topology:H) Recognise homotopy equivalence and say what a space deformation-retracts to.
  - EK: Two spaces are homotopy equivalent when each maps to the other with both composites homotopic to the identity. A deformation retraction shrinks a space onto a subspace: an annulus or a Möbius band onto a circle, a disc onto a point.
  - Needs: TP-2.1.B, TP-4.1.B
  - P1 · drills: tp-homotopy-type, tp-homotopy-facts
- ● **TP-5.1.B** (cambridge-ii-algebraic-topology:H, cambridge-ii-algebraic-topology:K) Name the fundamental group of a standard space and find the rank of the fundamental group of a graph.
  - EK: π₁ is trivial for a sphere (dimension ≥ 2) or a disc, Z for the circle, Z² for the torus, Z/2 for the projective plane, and free on n generators for a wedge of n circles. A connected graph has free fundamental group of rank E − V + 1.
  - Needs: TP-5.1.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-graph-rank, tp-fundamental-groups
- ● **TP-5.1.C** (cambridge-ii-algebraic-topology:V) Count sheets of a covering and use the multiplicativity of Euler characteristic.
  - EK: An n-sheeted covering has χ(cover) = n χ(base) and corresponds to a subgroup of index n in the fundamental group of the base. The real line covers the circle with infinitely many sheets; the sphere covers the projective plane twice.
  - Needs: TP-5.1.B, TP-4.1.C
  - P1 · drills: tp-cover-genus, tp-covering-facts
- ● **TP-5.1.D** (cambridge-ii-algebraic-topology:L) Read Betti numbers and Euler characteristic and use the fixed-point theorems they give.
  - EK: χ = b₀ − b₁ + b₂ − … . A closed orientable surface of genus g has Betti numbers 1, 2g, 1; the sphere Sⁿ has homology only in degrees 0 and n. Brouwer: every continuous map of a closed ball to itself has a fixed point.
  - Needs: TP-5.1.B, TP-4.1.A
  - P1 · drills: tp-betti-one, tp-homology-facts
