## Big ideas

- **COUNT · Counting tells how many.** The last number said names the size of the set; numbers compare by matching or by place in the counting sequence, and addition and subtraction start as counting on and back.
- **PV · Base ten bundles by tens.** Every place is ten of the place to its right — ones, tens, hundreds, and to the right of the point tenths, hundredths, thousandths. Place value is what makes the algorithms work.
- **OPS · Operations have meanings and properties.** Each operation models situations (join, separate, compare, equal groups, arrays, scaling); the commutative, associative and distributive properties turn hard computations into easy ones and later become the rules of algebra.
- **LINE · One number line holds every number.** Whole numbers, fractions, decimals, negatives and irrationals are all points on one line; equivalence means the same point, order means left and right, and absolute value is distance from 0.
- **MULT · Multiplicative reasoning grows into proportion and function.** Comparing by "times as many" leads to fractions as scaling, ratios and unit rates, proportional relationships y = kx, slope, and linear functions y = mx + b.
- **MEAS · Measuring is iterating a unit.** Length, area, volume, angle, time and mass are counts of a chosen unit with no gaps or overlaps; area and volume are additive, and their formulas are multiplication of counted rows and layers.
- **SHAPE · Shapes are defined by attributes and moved by transformations.** Figures are classified by defining attributes in nested categories; rigid motions define congruence, dilations add similarity, and the Pythagorean Theorem links length and area.
- **DATA · Data vary, and variation can be summarized.** Data come from questions that anticipate variability; distributions have center, spread and shape; samples support inference about populations; probability measures chance from 0 to 1.

## Practices

- **MP1 · Make sense of problems and persevere in solving them.** Explain what the problem is asking, look for an entry point, plan a solution path, monitor progress and check whether the answer makes sense.
- **MP2 · Reason abstractly and quantitatively.** Decontextualize a situation into symbols and manipulate them; contextualize — say what the numbers and symbols mean in the situation.
- **MP3 · Construct viable arguments and critique the reasoning of others.** Justify conclusions from stated assumptions, definitions and earlier results; use counterexamples; decide whether another's argument makes sense.
- **MP4 · Model with mathematics.** Apply mathematics to everyday problems — equations, diagrams, tables, graphs, functions — and interpret the result in context.
- **MP5 · Use appropriate tools strategically.** Choose among concrete models, rulers, protractors, number lines, graphs and technology, knowing what each is good for and its limits.
- **MP6 · Attend to precision.** Use definitions, symbols (including =) and units precisely; label axes; calculate accurately and state answers with appropriate precision.
- **MP7 · Look for and make use of structure.** See patterns and structure — 7 × 8 = 7 × 5 + 7 × 3, x² + 9x + 14 as a product, a sum as one object — and use them.
- **MP8 · Look for and express regularity in repeated reasoning.** Notice when calculations repeat, look for general methods and shortcuts, and check intermediate results while keeping the whole process in view.

## Unit 1 · Kindergarten — counting, adding and subtracting within 10, shapes

Big ideas: COUNT, OPS, MEAS, SHAPE

### 1.1 Counting and Cardinality



- ○ **MK-1.1.A** (K.CC.A.1) Count to 100 by ones and by tens.
  - EK: The counting sequence follows a repeating pattern of ones inside a pattern of tens.
  - MP7
- ○ **MK-1.1.B** (K.CC.A.2) Count forward beginning from a given number within the known sequence (instead of having to begin at 1).
  - EK: Any number in the sequence can be a starting point; the next number is always one more.
  - Needs: MK-1.1.A
  - MP7
- ○ **MK-1.1.C** (K.CC.A.3) Write numbers from 0 to 20 and represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0–20 (0 = no objects).
  - EK: A numeral is a symbol for how many; 0 names an empty set.
  - Needs: MK-1.1.A
  - MP6
- ○ **MK-1.1.D** (K.CC.B.4a, K.CC.B.4b, K.CC.B.4c) Count objects by saying the number names in standard order, pairing each object with one and only one number name.
  - EK: One-to-one correspondence; the last number said is how many (cardinality), whatever the order counted; each successive number is one larger.
  - Needs: MK-1.1.A
  - MP2 · MP6
- ○ **MK-1.1.E** (K.CC.B.5) Count to answer "how many?" for up to 20 objects in a line, array or circle (10 scattered), and count out that many objects given a number 1–20.
  - EK: Arrangement does not change how many; scattered sets need a way to track what was counted.
  - Needs: MK-1.1.D
  - MP6
- ○ **MK-1.1.F** (K.CC.C.6) Identify whether the number of objects in one group is greater than, less than, or equal to the number in another group (up to 10).

  - Needs: MK-1.1.E
  - MP2 · MP3
- ○ **MK-1.1.G** (K.CC.C.7) Compare two numbers between 1 and 10 presented as written numerals.
