## Big ideas

- **FORM · Structure fits function.** A thin flat cell is for exchange, a thick wall for pressure, a folded surface for area. Naming a structure is half of knowing what it does.
- **HOME · Homeostasis by feedback.** A sensor, a control centre and an effector hold a variable near its set point; most loops are negative and oppose the change.
- **GRAD · Flow follows gradients.** Blood, air, filtrate, ions and water move down pressure, concentration and electrical gradients, and the amount moved is the gradient over the resistance.

## Practices

- **P1 · Compute.** Carry a physiological relation to a number with its unit.
- **P2 · Identify.** Name the structure, the step or the cause from a description.

### 1.2 Homeostasis



- ● **HP-1.2.A** (haps-ap-2019:B) Name the parts of a feedback loop and tell negative from positive feedback.
  - EK: A receptor senses the variable, a control centre compares it with a set point, and an effector acts. Negative feedback reverses the change (temperature, glucose, blood pressure); positive feedback amplifies it to an end point (labour, clotting, the upstroke of an action potential).

### 4.1 Contraction



- ● **HP-4.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:G) Describe the sarcomere and what changes when it shortens.
  - EK: A sarcomere runs from Z disc to Z disc. Thin (actin) filaments slide past thick (myosin) filaments: the I band and H zone narrow and the Z discs approach, while the A band keeps its length because no filament shortens.

  - EK: Acetylcholine released at the neuromuscular junction opens ligand-gated channels on the motor end plate; the action potential runs down the T tubules and opens the sarcoplasmic reticulum's calcium channels. ATP is needed to detach the myosin head and to pump calcium back.

  - EK: A motor unit is one motor neuron and the fibres it supplies. Force rises by recruiting more units and by stimulating faster (wave summation up to tetanus). Slow oxidative fibres resist fatigue; fast glycolytic fibres are powerful and tire quickly.

## Unit 5 · Nervous system

Big ideas: GRAD, HOME

### 5.1 Signals



- ● **HP-5.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:H) Explain the resting potential and compute an equilibrium potential.
  - EK: The resting membrane is most permeable to potassium, so it sits near the potassium equilibrium potential, about −70 mV; the sodium-potassium pump maintains the gradients. At 37 °C the Nernst potential for a monovalent cation is 61.5 mV × log10(outside/inside).
  - Needs: HP-4.2.A, BI-2.9.B
  - P1 · drills: hp-nernst, hp-resting-potential
- ● **HP-5.1.B** (haps-ap-2019:H) Follow the phases of an action potential and its conduction.
  - EK: At threshold voltage-gated sodium channels open (depolarisation), then inactivate while potassium channels open (repolarisation, then hyperpolarisation). The absolute refractory period makes conduction one-way. Myelin and large diameter raise speed; myelinated conduction is saltatory.
  - Needs: HP-5.1.A
  - P1 · drills: hp-action-potential, hp-conduction-time
- ● **HP-5.1.C** (haps-ap-2019:H) Describe a chemical synapse and tell excitatory from inhibitory effects.
  - EK: An action potential opens voltage-gated calcium channels in the terminal; vesicles release transmitter, which binds postsynaptic receptors. Opening cation channels gives an EPSP; opening chloride or potassium channels gives an IPSP. Potentials sum in time and space at the axon hillock.
  - Needs: HP-5.1.B
  - P2 · drills: hp-synapse-steps, hp-epsp-ipsp

- ● **HP-5.2.A** (haps-ap-2019:H) Match brain regions to their functions.
  - EK: Frontal lobe: motor cortex and planning; parietal: somatosensory; occipital: vision; temporal: hearing and memory. The cerebellum coordinates movement, the hypothalamus runs homeostasis and the pituitary, the thalamus relays sensation, the medulla holds cardiac and respiratory centres.

Dorsal columns carry fine touch and proprioception, the spinothalamic tract pain and temperature, the corticospinal tract voluntary movement.

- ● **HP-5.2.C** (haps-ap-2019:H) Name the cranial nerves by number and main function.

## Unit 7 · Endocrine system

### 7.1 Hormones



- ● **HP-7.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:J) Classify hormones by solubility and predict where their receptors are.
  - EK: Peptides and catecholamines are water soluble, bind membrane receptors and act through second messengers within seconds to minutes. Steroids and thyroid hormones are lipid soluble, travel on carrier proteins, bind intracellular receptors and change gene transcription.
  - Needs: HP-5.3.A

- ● **HP-7.1.B** (haps-ap-2019:J) Follow a hypothalamic-pituitary axis and predict levels when one gland fails.
  - EK: The hypothalamus releases hormones into the portal system to the anterior pituitary (TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, GH, prolactin); the posterior pituitary releases ADH and oxytocin made in the hypothalamus. Target hormones feed back negatively, so a failing target gland gives a high tropic hormone.
  - Needs: HP-7.1.A

- ● **HP-7.1.C** (haps-ap-2019:J) Predict the hormonal response to a change in blood glucose.
  - EK: Insulin from beta cells lowers glucose by moving it into muscle and fat and storing it as glycogen and fat. Glucagon from alpha cells raises it by glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis in the liver; epinephrine, cortisol and growth hormone also raise it.
  - Needs: HP-7.1.B

## Unit 8 · Cardiovascular system

Big ideas: GRAD, HOME

### 8.1 Blood



- ● **HP-8.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:K) Describe the composition of blood and find the haematocrit.
  - EK: Blood is about 55% plasma and 45% formed elements. Haematocrit is the fraction of blood volume that is red cells. Erythrocytes carry gases, neutrophils are the commonest leukocyte, platelets are fragments of megakaryocytes.
  - Needs: HP-7.1.C
  - P1 · drills: hp-haematocrit, hp-formed-elements
- ● **HP-8.1.B** (haps-ap-2019:K) Order the steps of haemostasis.
  - EK: Vascular spasm, then a platelet plug on exposed collagen (with von Willebrand factor), then coagulation: the intrinsic and extrinsic pathways meet at factor X, prothrombin becomes thrombin and thrombin turns fibrinogen into fibrin. Plasmin later dissolves the clot.
  - Needs: HP-8.1.A
  - P2 · drills: hp-haemostasis-steps, hp-clotting-needs

### 8.2 Heart



- ● **HP-8.2.A** (haps-ap-2019:K) Trace blood through the heart and name the valves and sounds.
  - EK: Right atrium, tricuspid valve, right ventricle, pulmonary valve, lungs, left atrium, mitral valve, left ventricle, aortic valve, body. The first heart sound is the atrioventricular valves closing, the second the semilunar valves closing.
  - Needs: HP-8.1.B
  - P2 · drills: hp-heart-path, hp-heart-sounds
- ● **HP-8.2.B** (haps-ap-2019:K) Follow the conduction system and read the waves of an ECG.
  - EK: SA node, atria, AV node (delay), AV bundle, bundle branches, Purkinje fibres. The P wave is atrial depolarisation, the QRS complex ventricular depolarisation, the T wave ventricular repolarisation. Heart rate in beats per minute is 60 over the R-R interval in seconds.
  - Needs: HP-8.2.A
  - P1 · drills: hp-ecg-waves, hp-rate-from-rr
- ● **HP-8.2.C** (haps-ap-2019:K) Compute cardiac output, stroke volume and ejection fraction.
  - EK: Stroke volume is end-diastolic minus end-systolic volume; cardiac output is heart rate times stroke volume; ejection fraction is stroke volume over end-diastolic volume. Preload, contractility and afterload set stroke volume.
  - Needs: HP-8.2.B
  - P1 · drills: hp-cardiac-output, hp-ejection-fraction

### 8.3 Vessels and pressure



- ● **HP-8.3.A** (haps-ap-2019:K) Relate flow, pressure and resistance, and the effect of vessel radius.
  - EK: Flow equals the pressure difference over resistance, so mean arterial pressure is about cardiac output times total peripheral resistance. Resistance varies with the inverse fourth power of radius, which is why arterioles control flow.
  - Needs: HP-8.2.C
  - P1 · drills: hp-map-from-co, hp-radius-flow
- ● **HP-8.3.B** (haps-ap-2019:K) Find the net filtration pressure across a capillary wall.
  - EK: Net filtration pressure = (capillary hydrostatic − interstitial hydrostatic) − (plasma colloid osmotic − interstitial colloid osmotic). Positive values filter fluid out, negative values draw it in; the lymphatics return the excess.
  - Needs: HP-8.3.A
  - P1 · drills: hp-capillary-nfp, hp-oedema-causes
- ● **HP-8.3.C** (haps-ap-2019:K) Predict the reflex and hormonal responses to a change in blood pressure.
  - EK: Baroreceptors in the carotid sinus and aortic arch report to the medulla: a fall in pressure raises sympathetic output (heart rate, contractility, vasoconstriction). Over hours the kidney's renin starts the angiotensin-aldosterone system, and ADH retains water.
  - Needs: HP-8.3.B
  - P2 · drills: hp-baroreflex, hp-raas

## Unit 9 · Lymphatic system and immunity

### 9.1 Defence



- ● **HP-9.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:L) Trace lymph back to the blood and name the lymphoid organs.
  - EK: Lymphatic capillaries collect excess interstitial fluid; lymph passes through nodes to the thoracic duct (most of the body) or the right lymphatic duct, which empty into the subclavian veins. Bone marrow and thymus are primary lymphoid organs; nodes, spleen and tonsils are secondary.
  - Needs: HP-8.3.C

- ● **HP-9.1.B** (haps-ap-2019:L) Name the innate defences and the signs of inflammation.
  - EK: Barriers (skin, mucus, acid), phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages), natural killer cells, complement, interferons, fever and inflammation act without prior exposure. Inflammation shows redness, heat, swelling and pain from vasodilation and increased permeability.
  - Needs: HP-9.1.A
  - P2 · drills: hp-innate-cells, hp-inflammation
- ● **HP-9.1.C** (haps-ap-2019:L) Assign roles to the cells and antibodies of adaptive immunity.
  - EK: B cells become plasma cells that secrete antibody (humoral immunity). Helper T cells (CD4) read antigen on MHC II and direct the response; cytotoxic T cells (CD8) read MHC I and kill infected cells. IgM comes first, IgG is most abundant and crosses the placenta, IgA is in secretions, IgE drives allergy.
  - Needs: HP-9.1.B
  - P2 · drills: hp-adaptive-cells, hp-antibody-classes
- ● **HP-9.1.D** (haps-ap-2019:L) Tell primary from secondary responses and active from passive immunity.
  - EK: A first exposure gives a slow, small, mostly IgM response and leaves memory cells; a second gives a fast, large, mostly IgG response. Active immunity is made by one's own cells (infection, vaccine) and lasts; passive immunity is borrowed antibody (placenta, milk, antiserum) and fades.
  - Needs: HP-9.1.C
  - P2 · drills: hp-active-passive, hp-primary-secondary

## Unit 10 · Respiratory system

Big ideas: GRAD

### 10.1 Breathing and gas exchange



- ● **HP-10.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:M) Explain ventilation with Boyle's law.
  - EK: Contracting the diaphragm and external intercostals enlarges the thorax; alveolar pressure falls below atmospheric and air flows in. Quiet expiration is passive recoil. At constant temperature, pressure times volume is constant.
  - Needs: HP-8.3.C, CH-3.4.D
  - P1 · drills: hp-boyle-lung, hp-ventilation-mechanics
- ● **HP-10.1.B** (haps-ap-2019:M) Combine lung volumes and find alveolar ventilation.
  - EK: Vital capacity = tidal + inspiratory reserve + expiratory reserve volume; total lung capacity adds the residual volume. Minute ventilation is tidal volume times rate; alveolar ventilation subtracts the anatomical dead space (about 150 mL) from each breath.
  - Needs: HP-10.1.A
  - P1 · drills: hp-vital-capacity, hp-alveolar-ventilation
- ● **HP-10.1.C** (haps-ap-2019:M) Find partial pressures and predict shifts of the oxygen dissociation curve.
  - EK: A gas's partial pressure is its fraction times the total pressure of dry gas; inspired air is humidified, so subtract water vapour (47 mmHg at 37 °C). Raised carbon dioxide, acidity, temperature and 2,3-BPG shift the haemoglobin curve right, releasing more oxygen to tissue.
  - Needs: HP-10.1.B
  - P1 · drills: hp-inspired-po2, hp-o2-curve-shift
- ● **HP-10.1.D** (haps-ap-2019:M) Describe how carbon dioxide is carried and how breathing is controlled.
  - EK: Most carbon dioxide travels as bicarbonate made in red cells by carbonic anhydrase, some on haemoglobin, a little dissolved. Central chemoreceptors in the medulla respond to carbon dioxide through the pH of cerebrospinal fluid and set the normal drive; peripheral chemoreceptors respond to low oxygen.
  - Needs: HP-10.1.C
  - P2 · drills: hp-co2-transport, hp-breathing-control

### 11.1 Digestive system



- ● **HP-11.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:N) Name the layers of the gut wall and the types of motility.
  - EK: From the lumen out: mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa (inner circular, outer longitudinal), serosa. The submucosal and myenteric plexuses form the enteric nervous system. Peristalsis propels; segmentation mixes.

  - EK: Salivary and pancreatic amylase digest starch; pepsin (from chief cells' pepsinogen, activated by parietal cells' acid) starts protein digestion; pancreatic trypsin, lipase and brush-border enzymes finish; bile salts emulsify fat.

- ● **HP-11.1.C** (haps-ap-2019:N) Describe absorption and the roles of the liver, gallbladder and pancreas.
  - EK: Villi and microvilli give the small intestine its area; sugars and amino acids enter capillaries to the hepatic portal vein, fats enter lacteals as chylomicrons. Secretin calls for pancreatic bicarbonate; cholecystokinin calls for enzymes and gallbladder contraction.
  - Needs: HP-11.1.B
  - P2 · drills: hp-absorption, hp-gi-hormones

### 11.2 Nutrients and energy



- ● **HP-11.2.A** (haps-ap-2019:O) Count the energy in a meal and say which hormone governs each metabolic state.
  - EK: Carbohydrate and protein give about 4 kcal per gram, fat about 9. In the absorptive state insulin stores glucose as glycogen and fat; in the postabsorptive state glucagon releases glucose from glycogen and by gluconeogenesis, and fat is mobilised.
  - Needs: HP-11.1.C

## Unit 12 · Urinary system and fluid balance

Big ideas: GRAD, HOME

### 12.1 Kidney



- ● **HP-12.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:P) Order the parts of the nephron and name its three processes.
  - EK: Glomerulus and capsule, proximal convoluted tubule, nephron loop, distal convoluted tubule, collecting duct. Filtration moves plasma into the capsule, reabsorption returns most of it to blood (about two-thirds in the proximal tubule), secretion adds to the tubule.
  - Needs: HP-8.3.C, HP-10.1.D, CH-8.9.C
  - P2 · drills: hp-nephron-order, hp-renal-process
- ● **HP-12.1.B** (haps-ap-2019:P) Find the glomerular net filtration pressure and a renal clearance.
  - EK: Net filtration pressure = glomerular hydrostatic − capsular hydrostatic − glomerular colloid osmotic pressure. Clearance of a substance = urine concentration × urine flow ÷ plasma concentration; for inulin or creatinine it estimates GFR.
  - Needs: HP-12.1.A
  - P1 · drills: hp-glomerular-nfp, hp-clearance
- ● **HP-12.1.C** (haps-ap-2019:P) Compute filtered load and filtration fraction, and place the actions of ADH and aldosterone.
  - EK: Filtered load = GFR × plasma concentration; filtration fraction = GFR ÷ renal plasma flow, about 20%. ADH inserts aquaporins in the collecting duct to retain water; aldosterone raises sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion in the distal nephron.
  - Needs: HP-12.1.B
  - P1 · drills: hp-filtered-load, hp-filtration-fraction

### 12.2 Fluids and acid-base



- ● **HP-12.2.A** (haps-ap-2019:Q) Divide body water into compartments and estimate plasma osmolarity.
  - EK: Total body water is about 60% of body mass; two-thirds is intracellular and one-third extracellular, and about a quarter of the extracellular fluid is plasma. Plasma osmolarity ≈ 2 × sodium + glucose/18 + urea nitrogen/2.8 (mEq/L, mg/dL, mg/dL).
  - Needs: HP-12.1.C
  - P1 · drills: hp-icf-volume, hp-plasma-osmolarity
- ● **HP-12.2.B** (haps-ap-2019:Q) Compute blood pH from the bicarbonate buffer and the anion gap.
  - EK: pH = 6.1 + log10(bicarbonate ÷ (0.03 × carbon dioxide partial pressure)); the lungs set carbon dioxide and the kidneys set bicarbonate. Anion gap = sodium − chloride − bicarbonate, normally about 8 to 12 mEq/L.
  - Needs: HP-12.2.A
  - P1 · drills: hp-henderson-hasselbalch, hp-anion-gap

### 13.1 Reproduction



- ● **HP-13.1.A** (haps-ap-2019:R) Follow gametogenesis and the path of sperm.
  - EK: Spermatogenesis in the seminiferous tubules yields four sperm per primary spermatocyte, supported by sustentacular cells; interstitial cells make testosterone under LH. Oogenesis yields one ovum and polar bodies and pauses in prophase I until puberty and in metaphase II until fertilisation.
  - Needs: HP-7.1.C
  - P2 · drills: hp-gametogenesis, hp-sperm-path
- ● **HP-13.1.B** (haps-ap-2019:R) Time the events and hormones of the ovarian and uterine cycles.
  - EK: FSH grows follicles, which secrete oestrogen (proliferative phase). An LH surge near day 14 triggers ovulation. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone (secretory phase); without pregnancy it regresses, hormones fall and menstruation begins. hCG from an embryo rescues the corpus luteum.
  - Needs: HP-13.1.A
  - P2 · drills: hp-ovarian-cycle, hp-pregnancy-hormones
- ● **HP-13.1.C** (haps-ap-2019:T) Order early development and assign tissues to germ layers.
  - EK: Zygote, cleavage, morula, blastocyst (implants about a week after fertilisation), then gastrulation forms ectoderm (nervous system, epidermis), mesoderm (muscle, bone, blood, heart, kidney) and endoderm (lining of gut and airways, liver, pancreas).
  - Needs: HP-13.1.B
  - P2 · drills: hp-germ-layers, hp-early-development
