## Big ideas

- **EQUIV · Equivalent forms reveal structure.** An expression or equation can be rewritten without changing what it says; factored, vertex, exponential-rate and polar forms each expose a different property, and solving is a chain of steps that keep the solution set the same.
- **FUNC · Functions relate quantities.** A function gives each input one output; formulas, tables, graphs and words are representations of one object, and each family (linear, exponential, quadratic, polynomial, rational, logarithmic, periodic) is recognised by how it changes.
- **NUM · Number systems extend.** Integers → rationals → reals → complex numbers, then vectors and matrices; each extension keeps the properties of operations it can and is built so that old rules (exponents, factoring) still hold.
- **XFORM · Geometry through transformations and coordinates.** Congruence is defined by rigid motions and similarity by dilations; trigonometry is similarity of right triangles, and coordinates turn geometric claims into algebra.
- **MODEL · Modeling is a cycle.** Formulate a model from a situation, compute with it, interpret the result in context, and validate it against the situation; choose the function family from how the quantity changes.
- **DATA · Data vary, and chance can be quantified.** Distributions are described by shape, center and spread; probability measures chance; randomisation in sampling and experiments is what licenses conclusions about populations and causes.

## Practices

- **MP1 · Make sense of problems and persevere in solving them.** Explain what the problem asks, plan a route, try simpler cases, and check whether the answer makes sense.
- **MP2 · Reason abstractly and quantitatively.** Decontextualise a situation into symbols and recontextualise the result; keep track of what each quantity and unit means.
- **MP3 · Construct viable arguments and critique the reasoning of others.** Build a proof or justification from definitions and earlier results; find the flaw in an argument.
- **MP4 · Model with mathematics.** Turn a real situation into an equation, function, figure or statistical model, and judge whether its answer fits.
- **MP5 · Use appropriate tools strategically.** Choose among paper, graphing technology, spreadsheets, construction tools and simulation, and know each one's limits.
- **MP6 · Attend to precision.** State definitions exactly, label units and axes, and report answers to the precision the data allow.
- **MP7 · Look for and make use of structure.** See a complicated expression or figure as built from simpler parts, e.g. a quadratic in x², or an auxiliary line.
- **MP8 · Look for and express regularity in repeated reasoning.** Notice when a calculation repeats and generalise it into a rule or formula (slope, series sums, recursion).

## Unit 1 · Relationships between quantities and reasoning with equations

Big ideas: EQUIV, MODEL

### 1.1 Reason quantitatively and use units to solve problems



- ○ **MH-1.1.A** (N-Q.A.1) Use units to choose and check the solution path of a multi-step problem, converting units and choosing scales for graphs.
  - EK: Units multiply and divide like factors; a result in the wrong units is wrong.
  - MP2 · MP6
- ○ **MH-1.1.B** (N-Q.A.2) Define the quantities and variables needed to describe a situation.
  - EK: A quantity is a number with a unit attached to a specific attribute of a specific object.
  - Needs: MH-1.1.A
  - MP2 · MP4
- ○ **MH-1.1.C** (N-Q.A.3) Report a computed quantity to a level of accuracy that matches the limitations of the measurements it came from.
  - EK: A result is no more precise than the least precise measurement that went into it.
  - Needs: MH-1.1.A
  - MP6

### 1.2 Interpret the structure of expressions



- ○ **MH-1.2.A** (A-SSE.A.1a) Identify the terms, factors and coefficients of an expression and say what each means in context.
  - EK: Terms are added, factors are multiplied; a coefficient scales its variable.
  - MP2 · MP7
- ○ **MH-1.2.B** (A-SSE.A.1b) Interpret a complicated expression by viewing one or more of its parts as a single entity, e.g. P(1 + r)^n as the product of P and a factor not depending on P.
  - EK: Treating a sub-expression as one object shows the role it plays in the whole.
  - Needs: MH-1.2.A
  - MP7

### 1.3 Solve equations and inequalities in one variable as a process of reasoning



- ○ **MH-1.3.A** (A-REI.A.1) Explain each step in solving a simple equation as following from the equality asserted in the previous step, and justify the method.
  - EK: Applying the same invertible operation to both sides keeps the solution set unchanged.
  - MP3 · MP7
- ○ **MH-1.3.B** (A-REI.B.3) Solve a linear equation in one variable, including equations with coefficients represented by letters.
  - EK: Collect the variable terms on one side and constants on the other, then divide by the (nonzero) coefficient.
  - Needs: MH-1.3.A
  - MP6 · MP7
- ○ **MH-1.3.C** (A-REI.B.3) Solve a linear inequality in one variable and write the solution set.
  - EK: Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality.
  - Needs: MH-1.3.B
  - MP6

### 1.4 Create equations that describe numbers or relationships



- ○ **MH-1.4.A** (A-CED.A.1) Write and solve a one-variable linear equation or inequality that represents a word problem.
  - EK: Name the unknown, translate each sentence into a relation, solve, then check the answer in the words.
  - Needs: MH-1.1.B, MH-1.3.C
  - MP4 · MP2
- ○ **MH-1.4.B** (A-CED.A.2) Write an equation in two variables that relates two quantities and graph it on coordinate axes with labels and scales.
  - EK: Each variable is one quantity with a unit; the axes carry those units.
  - Needs: MH-1.4.A
  - MP4 · MP6
- ○ **MH-1.4.C** (A-CED.A.3) Represent constraints by equations or inequalities, or by systems of them, and decide whether a solution is viable in context.
  - EK: A mathematical solution can be non-viable: negative counts, fractional people, or values outside the constraints.
  - Needs: MH-1.4.A
  - MP4
- ○ **MH-1.4.D** (A-CED.A.4) Rearrange a formula to highlight a quantity of interest, e.g. Ohm's law V = IR for R.

  - Needs: MH-1.3.B
  - MP7

## Unit 2 · Linear and exponential relationships

Big ideas: FUNC, MODEL, EQUIV

### 2.1 Understand the concept of a function and use function notation



- ○ **MH-2.1.A** (F-IF.A.1) Decide whether a relation given by a table, graph, mapping or rule is a function, and state its domain and range.
  - EK: A function assigns to each input exactly one output; its graph is the set of points (x, f(x)).
  - MP6
- ○ **MH-2.1.B** (F-IF.A.2) Evaluate a function written in function notation for given inputs and interpret f(a) in context.
  - EK: f(a) is the output when the input is a; f(a + 1) is not f(a) + 1.
  - Needs: MH-2.1.A
  - MP2 · MP6
- ○ **MH-2.1.C** (F-IF.A.3) Recognise a sequence as a function whose domain is a subset of the integers, and generate terms from a recursive definition.
  - EK: A recursive rule gives each term from the one before, e.g. f(0) = f(1) = 1, f(n + 1) = f(n) + f(n − 1).
  - Needs: MH-2.1.B
  - MP8

### 2.2 Interpret functions that arise in applications in terms of a context



- ○ **MH-2.2.A** (F-IF.B.4) Interpret key features of a graph or table in context (intercepts, intervals of increase and decrease, where positive or negative, maxima and minima), and sketch a graph from a verbal description.
  - EK: Each graphical feature is a statement about the quantities: an intercept is a starting value, a high point is a largest value.
  - Needs: MH-2.1.B
  - MP2 · MP4
- ○ **MH-2.2.B** (F-IF.B.5) Relate the domain of a function to its graph and to the quantity it describes.
  - EK: Context restricts the domain, e.g. whole numbers for a count of items, t ≥ 0 for time.
  - Needs: MH-2.2.A
  - MP2
- ○ **MH-2.2.C** (F-IF.B.6) Calculate and interpret the average rate of change of a function over an interval from a formula, a table or a graph.
  - EK: Average rate of change = (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a), the slope of the secant line, in output units per input unit.
  - Needs: MH-2.2.A
  - MP2 · MP6

### 2.3 Graphs of equations and linear functions



- ○ **MH-2.3.A** (A-REI.D.10) Explain that the graph of an equation in two variables is the set of all its solutions plotted in the coordinate plane.
  - EK: A point lies on the graph exactly when its coordinates satisfy the equation.
  - Needs: MH-1.4.B
  - MP3
- ○ **MH-2.3.B** (F-IF.C.7a) Graph a linear function from its equation, showing the intercepts and the slope.
  - EK: y = mx + b: slope m = rise/run, y-intercept b, x-intercept −b/m.
  - Needs: MH-2.3.A, MH-2.2.C
  - MP5 · MP6

### 2.4 Distinguish linear from exponential change



- ○ **MH-2.4.A** (F-LE.A.1a) Prove that linear functions grow by equal differences over equal intervals and exponential functions grow by equal factors over equal intervals.
  - EK: f(x + Δ) − f(x) = mΔ for linear; f(x + Δ)/f(x) = b^Δ for exponential.
  - Needs: MH-2.2.C
  - MP3 · MP8
- ○ **MH-2.4.B** (F-LE.A.1b) Recognise a situation in which one quantity changes at a constant rate per unit interval relative to another.
  - EK: Constant amount added per step ⇒ linear.
  - Needs: MH-2.4.A
  - MP4
- ○ **MH-2.4.C** (F-LE.A.1c) Recognise a situation in which a quantity grows or decays by a constant percent rate per unit interval.
  - EK: Constant percent change r per step ⇒ multiply by (1 + r) each step ⇒ exponential.
  - Needs: MH-2.4.A
  - MP4

### 2.5 Build linear and exponential functions and sequences



- ○ **MH-2.5.A** (F-LE.A.2) Construct a linear or exponential function, including arithmetic and geometric sequences, from a graph, a description of a relationship, or two input–output pairs.
  - EK: Linear: m = Δy/Δx, then b; exponential through (x₁, y₁), (x₂, y₂): b = (y₂/y₁)^(1/(x₂ − x₁)), then a.
  - Needs: MH-2.4.B, MH-2.4.C, MH-2.3.B
  - MP4 · MP7
- ○ **MH-2.5.B** (F-BF.A.2) Write an arithmetic or geometric sequence both recursively and with an explicit formula, and translate between the two forms.
  - EK: a_n = a₁ + (n − 1)d ⇔ a_n = a_(n−1) + d; a_n = a₁r^(n−1) ⇔ a_n = r·a_(n−1).
  - Needs: MH-2.1.C, MH-2.5.A
  - MP8
- ○ **MH-2.5.C** (F-BF.A.1a) Determine an explicit expression, a recursive process, or steps for calculation from a context.
  - EK: Describe what happens to the quantity at each step; that description is the recursion, and repeating it gives the explicit formula.
  - Needs: MH-2.5.B
  - MP4 · MP8
- ○ **MH-2.5.D** (F-LE.B.5) Interpret the parameters of a linear or exponential function in terms of a context.
  - EK: y = mx + b: m is the rate, b the initial value; y = a·b^x: a is the initial value, b − 1 the percent change per unit.
  - Needs: MH-2.5.A
  - MP2 · MP4

### 2.6 Rational exponents and exponential graphs



- ○ **MH-2.6.A** (N-RN.A.1) Explain how the meaning of rational exponents follows from extending the properties of integer exponents, e.g. why 5^(1/3) is the cube root of 5.
  - EK: (5^(1/3))³ = 5^((1/3)·3) = 5, so 5^(1/3) must be a cube root of 5.
  - MP3 · MP7
- ○ **MH-2.6.B** (N-RN.A.2) Rewrite expressions involving radicals and rational exponents using the properties of exponents.
  - EK: x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)^m; a^p·a^q = a^(p+q); (a^p)^q = a^(pq); a^(−p) = 1/a^p.
  - Needs: MH-2.6.A
  - MP7
- ○ **MH-2.6.C** (F-IF.C.7e) Graph an exponential function y = a·b^x, showing the intercept and end behaviour.
  - EK: Passes through (0, a); grows if b > 1, decays if 0 < b < 1; horizontal asymptote y = 0.
  - Needs: MH-2.5.A
  - MP5 · MP6
- ○ **MH-2.6.D** (F-LE.A.3) Show with graphs and tables that a quantity increasing exponentially eventually exceeds one increasing linearly.
  - EK: Equal factors eventually beat equal differences, however small the base above 1.
  - Needs: MH-2.6.C
  - MP5 · MP8
- ○ **MH-2.6.E** (F-IF.C.9) Compare properties of two functions each represented in a different way (algebraically, graphically, numerically in a table, or by a verbal description).
  - EK: Read the same feature (rate, intercept, maximum) from each representation before comparing.
  - Needs: MH-2.6.C
  - MP2 · MP7

### 2.7 Solve systems of equations and represent inequalities graphically



- ○ **MH-2.7.A** (A-REI.C.6) Solve a system of two linear equations approximately by graphing.
  - EK: The solution is the intersection point; parallel lines give no solution, coincident lines infinitely many.
  - Needs: MH-2.3.B
  - MP5
- ○ **MH-2.7.B** (A-REI.C.6) Solve a system of two linear equations exactly by substitution or elimination.
  - EK: Eliminate one variable to get a one-variable equation, solve, then back-substitute.
  - Needs: MH-2.7.A, MH-1.3.B
  - MP6 · MP7
- ○ **MH-2.7.C** (A-REI.C.5) Prove that replacing one equation of a system by the sum of that equation and a multiple of the other gives a system with the same solutions.
  - EK: Any (x, y) satisfying both original equations satisfies the combination, and the step can be undone.
  - Needs: MH-2.7.B
  - MP3
- ○ **MH-2.7.D** (A-REI.D.11) Explain why the x-coordinates of the intersections of y = f(x) and y = g(x) solve f(x) = g(x), and approximate them with graphs or tables (linear and exponential f, g).
  - EK: At an intersection both outputs are equal for the same input.
  - Needs: MH-2.7.A, MH-2.6.C
  - MP3 · MP5
- ○ **MH-2.7.E** (A-REI.D.12) Graph the solutions of a linear inequality in two variables as a half-plane, and of a system of linear inequalities as the intersection of half-planes.
  - EK: Dashed boundary for < or >, solid for ≤ or ≥; test a point to choose the side.
  - Needs: MH-2.7.A, MH-1.3.C
  - MP5 · MP6

## Unit 3 · Descriptive statistics

Big ideas: DATA, MODEL

### 3.1 Summarize, represent and interpret data on a single count or measurement variable



- ○ **MH-3.1.A** (S-ID.A.1) Represent data with plots on the real number line: dot plots, histograms and box plots.
  - EK: A box plot shows the five-number summary: minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum.
  - MP5 · MP6
- ○ **MH-3.1.B** (S-ID.A.2) Compare the center (median, mean) and spread (interquartile range, standard deviation) of two or more data sets, using statistics suited to their shape.
  - EK: Skewed data or outliers → median and IQR; roughly symmetric → mean and standard deviation.
  - Needs: MH-3.1.A
  - MP2 · MP3
- ○ **MH-3.1.C** (S-ID.A.3) Interpret differences in shape, center and spread in context, accounting for possible effects of extreme data points (outliers).
  - EK: An outlier lies more than 1.5·IQR beyond a quartile; it moves the mean and SD much more than the median and IQR.
  - Needs: MH-3.1.B
  - MP2 · MP3

### 3.2 Summarize, represent and interpret data on two categorical and quantitative variables



- ○ **MH-3.2.A** (S-ID.B.5) Summarise categorical data for two categories in two-way frequency tables and interpret joint, marginal and conditional relative frequencies.
  - EK: Joint = cell / grand total; marginal = row or column total / grand total; conditional = cell / its row or column total.
  - MP2 · MP6
- ○ **MH-3.2.B** (S-ID.B.6a) Fit a function (linear, quadratic or exponential) to data on two quantitative variables in a scatter plot and use it to solve problems in the context of the data.
  - EK: Pick the family from the shape of the scatter and the context, then use technology to fit its parameters.
  - Needs: MH-2.5.A
  - MP4 · MP5
- ○ **MH-3.2.C** (S-ID.B.6b) Informally assess the fit of a function by plotting and analysing residuals.
  - EK: Residual = observed − predicted; a good fit leaves residuals scattered about 0 with no pattern.
  - Needs: MH-3.2.B
  - MP4 · MP5
- ○ **MH-3.2.D** (S-ID.B.6c) Fit a linear function to a scatter plot that suggests a linear association.
  - EK: The least-squares line passes through (x̄, ȳ) and minimises the sum of squared residuals.
  - Needs: MH-3.2.B
  - MP4 · MP5

### 3.3 Interpret linear models



- ○ **MH-3.3.A** (S-ID.C.7) Interpret the slope (rate of change) and intercept (constant term) of a linear model in the context of the data.
  - EK: Slope = predicted change in y per one-unit increase in x; the intercept may be meaningless outside the data range.
  - Needs: MH-3.2.D, MH-2.5.D
  - MP2 · MP4
- ○ **MH-3.3.B** (S-ID.C.8) Compute (using technology) and interpret the correlation coefficient of a linear fit.
  - EK: −1 ≤ r ≤ 1; the sign gives direction, |r| near 1 a strong linear association.
  - Needs: MH-3.3.A
  - MP5 · MP6
- ○ **MH-3.3.C** (S-ID.C.9) Distinguish between correlation and causation.
  - EK: Only a randomised experiment supports a causal claim; an association can come from a lurking variable.
  - Needs: MH-3.3.B
  - MP3

## Unit 4 · Expressions and equations

Big ideas: EQUIV, NUM

### 4.1 Perform arithmetic on polynomials and use structure



- ○ **MH-4.1.A** (A-APR.A.1) Add, subtract and multiply polynomials, and explain why the result is again a polynomial.
  - EK: Polynomials are closed under +, − and ×, like the integers; multiply by distributing every term over every term.
  - Needs: MH-1.2.A
  - MP6 · MP7
- ○ **MH-4.1.B** (A-SSE.A.2) Use the structure of an expression to rewrite it, e.g. x⁴ − y⁴ as (x²)² − (y²)² = (x² − y²)(x² + y²).
  - EK: Recognise a² − b², a² ± 2ab + b² and common factors, where a and b may themselves be expressions.
  - Needs: MH-4.1.A, MH-1.2.B
  - MP7

### 4.2 Write expressions in equivalent forms to solve problems



- ○ **MH-4.2.A** (A-SSE.B.3a) Factor a quadratic expression to reveal the zeros of the function it defines.
  - EK: ax² + bx + c = a(x − r)(x − s) ⇒ zeros at x = r and x = s.
  - Needs: MH-4.1.B
  - MP7
- ○ **MH-4.2.B** (A-SSE.B.3b) Complete the square in a quadratic expression to reveal the maximum or minimum value of the function it defines.
  - EK: ax² + bx + c = a(x − h)² + k with h = −b/2a; the extreme value is k, reached at x = h.
  - Needs: MH-4.1.A
  - MP7
- ○ **MH-4.2.C** (A-SSE.B.3c) Use the properties of exponents to transform an exponential expression, e.g. 1.15^t ≈ 1.012^(12t), to reveal an equivalent monthly rate.
  - EK: b^t = (b^(1/k))^(kt): the same growth expressed per 1/k of the unit.
  - Needs: MH-2.6.B
  - MP7

### 4.3 Solve quadratic equations in one variable



- ○ **MH-4.3.A** (A-REI.B.4b) Solve a quadratic equation by inspection, by taking square roots, or by factoring.
  - EK: Zero-product property: ab = 0 ⇒ a = 0 or b = 0; x² = k ⇒ x = ±√k.
  - Needs: MH-4.2.A
  - MP7
- ○ **MH-4.3.B** (A-REI.B.4a) Complete the square to transform a quadratic equation into the form (x − p)² = q, and derive the quadratic formula from that form.
  - EK: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a.
  - Needs: MH-4.2.B, MH-4.3.A
  - MP3 · MP7
- ○ **MH-4.3.C** (A-REI.B.4b) Solve a quadratic equation with the quadratic formula, choosing the method that fits its form, and use the discriminant to recognise when it has no real solutions.
  - EK: b² − 4ac > 0: two real roots; = 0: one repeated root; < 0: no real roots (complex a ± bi).
  - Needs: MH-4.3.B
  - MP6 · MP7
- ○ **MH-4.3.D** (A-CED.A.1) Write and solve a quadratic equation that models a word problem, rejecting solutions that do not fit the context.
  - EK: A negative time or length from the formula is a mathematical root, not an answer.
  - Needs: MH-4.3.C, MH-1.4.A
  - MP4

### 4.4 Solve linear–quadratic systems



- ○ **MH-4.4.A** (A-REI.C.7) Solve a system of one linear and one quadratic equation in two variables algebraically and graphically, e.g. find where y = −3x meets x² + y² = 3.
  - EK: Substitute the linear equation into the quadratic; 0, 1 or 2 real solutions match 0, 1 or 2 intersection points.
  - Needs: MH-4.3.C, MH-2.7.B
  - MP5 · MP7

## Unit 5 · Quadratic functions and modeling

Big ideas: FUNC, EQUIV, NUM, MODEL

### 5.1 Use properties of rational and irrational numbers



- ○ **MH-5.1.A** (N-RN.B.3) Explain why the sum or product of two rational numbers is rational, the sum of a rational and an irrational number is irrational, and the product of a nonzero rational and an irrational number is irrational.
  - EK: If r + x were rational for rational r, then x = (r + x) − r would be rational too.
  - Needs: MH-4.3.C
  - MP3

### 5.2 Analyze quadratic and related functions using different representations



- ○ **MH-5.2.A** (F-IF.C.8a) Rewrite a quadratic function by factoring and completing the square to show its zeros, extreme value and symmetry, and interpret them in context.
  - EK: Factored form shows zeros; vertex form a(x − h)² + k shows the extreme value k and axis x = h.
  - Needs: MH-4.2.A, MH-4.2.B
  - MP7 · MP2
- ○ **MH-5.2.B** (F-IF.C.7a) Graph a quadratic function, showing intercepts, the vertex (maximum or minimum) and the axis of symmetry.
  - EK: Vertex at x = −b/2a; opens up if a > 0, down if a < 0; y-intercept c.
  - Needs: MH-5.2.A, MH-2.3.B
  - MP5 · MP6
- ○ **MH-5.2.C** (F-IF.B.4) Interpret the key features of a quadratic model in context, e.g. the maximum height and landing time of a projectile.
  - EK: The vertex gives the extreme value and when it happens; the zeros give when the quantity is zero.
  - Needs: MH-5.2.B, MH-2.2.A
  - MP2 · MP4
- ○ **MH-5.2.D** (F-IF.C.7b) Graph square-root, cube-root and piecewise-defined functions, including step functions and absolute-value functions.
  - EK: |x| = x for x ≥ 0 and −x for x < 0; √x needs x ≥ 0; each piece applies only on its own interval.
  - Needs: MH-2.3.B, MH-2.6.B
  - MP5 · MP6
