## Big ideas

- **ROOT · The stance is the root.** Every technique is built on a standard base — foot angles, thigh height, weight split, alignment of knee over foot. Judges deduct, and stunts fail, at the base first.
- **WHOLE · Power is whole-body and sequenced.** Force comes from the floor, through legs and waist, out through the hands or weapon; hands, eyes, body and steps arrive together. Isolated limbs look weak on the carpet and on camera.
- **STYLE · Each style is a coherent set of principles.** Changquan (extension, speed, height), nanquan (rooted stances, bridge arms, short explosive power, sound) and taijiquan (continuity, softness, rising-sinking control) each answer "what is good movement" differently; weapons extend the same body method.
- **MECH · Jumps and spins obey mechanics.** Takeoff impulse fixes flight time; angular momentum is made on the floor and conserved in the air; shape changes trade inertia for spin; landings are impulse problems. Most faults are a mechanical cause, not a mystery.
- **SCORE · The score is an explicit model of quality.** Quality of movement (deduction codes), overall performance (graded bands) and degree of difficulty (coded elements and connections) make "good wushu" concrete and checkable.
- **SHOW · Performance communicates.** Rhythm, spirit (jingshen) and eyes (yanshen) turn correct technique into a performance; for screen work the same principles serve the camera, under strict partner safety.

## Practices

- **P1 · Name and classify.** Give a technique its Chinese (pinyin + characters) and English names, its family (stance, hand form, step, kick, balance, jump, tumbling, weapon method) and its code.
- **P2 · Observe and diagnose.** Compare a described or filmed execution to the standard and locate the fault precisely (which joint, which angle, which phase).
- **P3 · Apply the rules.** Assign deduction codes and values, difficulty and connection values, apply caps and judge-panel rules, and compute a score.
- **P4 · Explain the mechanism.** Give the biomechanical or stylistic reason a technique works or fails, and the change that fixes it.
- **P5 · Perform and self-assess.** Execute to standard, film it, check it against the criteria, and log the result.
- **P6 · Compose and adapt.** Build or edit a routine or fight phrase to a constraint — rules, music, carpet, camera, partner.

- ○ **WS-1.1.B** State the standard alignment of gongbu and mabu and name the deduction criteria that test it.

  - P1 · P3
- ○ **WS-1.1.C** State the standard alignment of xubu, pubu and xiebu and identify which standard a described stance breaks.
  - EK: Xubu: weight on the rear leg, rear thigh level, rear heel down (52).

  - EK: Xiebu: legs crossed, buttock resting on the rear calf (54).
  - P2 · P3
- ○ **WS-1.1.D** Hold each standard stance for time and film it front and side, checking every criterion.
  - EK: Stance holds (zhan zhuang) build the leg endurance that keeps thighs level late in a routine.

Book: iwuf-2024 §Art. 30 nanquan; iwuf-2019t §A·NQ 01–04, 20–42

## Unit 9 · Biomechanics and injury prevention

Big ideas: MECH, ROOT

### 9.1 Takeoff and flight

Book: bio-xfj §takeoff, §flight; bio-isbs §xuanfengjiao

- ○ **WS-9.1.A** Calculate flight time and jump height from takeoff vertical velocity (and the reverse) for a described jump.
  - EK: t_flight = 2v/g, h = v²/2g; 0.5 s of air needs v ≈ 2.45 m/s and h ≈ 0.31 m of CoM rise.
  - P4
- ○ **WS-9.1.B** Explain which takeoff changes raise flight time, and identify the change that fixes a described 'no height' fault.
  - EK: Vertical impulse ∫(F − mg)dt: a short, stiff ground contact with full hip–knee–ankle extension and an upward arm drive. bio-xfj: takeoff knee ≈ 60°, short foot-pat contact gives larger vertical acceleration.
  - P4

### 9.2 Rotation and angular momentum

Book: bio-ang §angular momentum model; bio-xfj §swivel

- ○ **WS-9.2.A** Explain where the angular momentum for a tornado kick or butterfly twist comes from and why it can't be added in the air.
  - EK: Friction torque from the feet on the floor (plus the arm and leg wind-up) generates L before takeoff; in flight no external torque acts about the CoM, so L is constant.
  - P4
- ○ **WS-9.2.B** Predict how a shape change in flight alters spin rate and identify which change fixes a described under-rotation.
  - EK: L = Iω constant: pulling arms and legs toward the spin axis cuts I and raises ω. Rotations = ω·t/2π, so under-rotation is fixed by more L at takeoff, a tighter shape, or more flight time.
  - Needs: WS-9.2.A, WS-9.1.A
  - P4
- ○ **WS-9.2.C** Explain why residual spin at landing causes shuffles and which landing shapes make it harder to kill.
  - EK: On touchdown the floor must apply an opposite torque; a wide base (mabu) supplies it easily, one foot or a split supplies little, so excess spin becomes a code-70 shuffle or a fall.
  - Needs: WS-9.2.A
  - P4
- ○ **WS-9.2.D** Explain the timing rule for the slap in xuanfengjiao and lotus kick and identify the fault when the slap is late.
  - EK: bio-xfj: the inward kick circle should be completed before the CoM peaks; slapping on the way down leaves no time to land and drops the slap below shoulder (code 30).
  - P2 · P4

### 9.3 Landing and balance

Book: bio-xfj §landing

- ○ **WS-9.3.A** Explain how a landing reduces peak force and identify the fix for a described 'stiff landing'.
  - EK: F_avg = Δp/Δt: sequential ankle–knee–hip flexion lengthens stopping time; landing stiff-legged spikes force on knees and spine.
  - P4
- ○ **WS-9.3.B** Explain why a balance holds or fails in terms of centre of mass and base of support.
  - EK: Static balance needs the CoM projection inside the base; single-leg balances (≥ 2 s, code 77) leave a base the size of one foot, so the trunk and raised leg must counter each other.
  - P4
- ○ **WS-9.3.C** Identify a knee-valgus or knee-over-toe misalignment in a described landing or stance and explain the injury risk.
  - EK: Knees caving inward on landing loads the ACL and MCL; the same alignment is judged in stances (mabu knees buckle in, code 51).
  - P2 · P4

### 9.4 Injury prevention and load management

Book: inj §results

- ○ **WS-9.4.A** State where wushu injuries concentrate and plan a prehab routine that targets those sites.
  - EK: Elite wushu study: 6.85 injuries / 1000 h, lower limb 47.6 %, then head/face (contact work); flat feet and shoulder asymmetry common. Ankle, knee and adductor/hamstring work goes first.
  - P5 · P6
- ○ **WS-9.4.B** Plan a weekly jump and landing volume with progressions and identify signs to back off.
  - EK: Landings are the dose: count them; progress rotation before landing difficulty; mats and lower-rotation drills carry the reps; pain on landing or loss of height signals fatigue.
  - P6
- ○ **WS-9.4.C** Explain how fatigue changes takeoff and landing mechanics and why difficulty goes early in a routine or take.
  - EK: Fatigue shortens flight, slows wind-up and softens landing control — all three raise the chance of 70–72 errors and injury.
  - Needs: WS-9.1.B, WS-9.3.A
  - P4
